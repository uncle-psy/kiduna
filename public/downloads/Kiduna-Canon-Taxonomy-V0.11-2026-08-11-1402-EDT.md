Kiduna Canon — Taxonomy
V0.11 — 2026-08-11 14:02 EDT
1. Categories
Kiduna is described through the following primary categories:
Domains — the four dimensions through which systems are designed and understood: Technology, Governance, Economics, and Culture.
Themes — fields of meaning that describe what a Realm concerns. Each Realm has one Primary Theme and one Primary Focus within that Theme, may have up to two Supporting Focuses, and can use open-ended Tags for additional specificity and discovery.
Forces — the dynamic influences that shape behavior, movement, relationship, attention, value, authority, persistence, and change throughout Kiduna.
Realms — the contexts in which Sources, Avatars, intelligence, relationships, resources, and activity come together.
Elements — the fundamental entities that exist and interact inside Kiduna.
Roles — Realm-scoped relationships and grants that define how an interaction is classified and how Sources participate, contribute, and exercise explicitly authorized privileges within a Realm.
Capacities — the foundational capabilities that shape what a Realm, Ally, or Actor knows, how it behaves, what it can access, what it can do, and how it remains coherent.
Patterns — reusable systems that organize activity for particular purposes.
Modes — the three concurrent ways of working with and experiencing the same underlying Realm or Scene: Forge, where changes are made; Rehearse, where Drafts are tested; and Field, where Sources participate in Final published experiences through their Avatars.
Scenes — situated compositions through which a Realm becomes present, experiential, and interactive.
Forms — the primary visual and interaction grammars through which Scenes are expressed.
Components — reusable visual and interactive building blocks used to construct Scenes.
Dimensions — the spatial systems through which Scenes and Components are represented, scaled, positioned, and related across 2D, 2.5D isometric, and 3D environments.
Designs — reusable visual systems that establish color, typography, materials, styling, and other presentation rules so Components and Scenes can remain coherent and compatible.
Surfaces — the device and experiential contexts through which Kiduna is experienced.

2. Domains
Domains are the four fundamental dimensions through which Kiduna systems are designed, understood, and developed. Every Realm can express all four: how it works, how it decides, how value flows, and how people relate.
Technology — capability – what can happen
The systems, intelligence, tools, infrastructure, protocols, data, and interfaces that determine what a Realm can do and how it operates.
Governance — authority – who decides
The structures and processes through which Sources establish rules, distribute authority, make decisions, grant permissions, resolve differences, and remain accountable.
Economics — value – how resources circulate
The ways resources are created, contributed, exchanged, allocated, compensated, held, and reinvested to sustain a Realm and its purpose.
Culture — meaning – how we belong
The values, relationships, language, practices, stories, aesthetics, norms, and shared experiences that shape how participation feels and what a Realm becomes.

3. Themes — Fields of Meaning
Themes describe what a Realm concerns. They help Sources discover, understand, and connect related Realms across Kiduna.
Themes do not determine a Realm’s type, structure, authority, visibility, governance, or behavior.
Theme
A Theme is a broad field of meaning that describes the general area a Realm concerns.
The six canonical Themes are:
People & Care · Society & Justice · Culture & Play · Place & Planet · Work & Wealth · Knowledge & Frontier
Focus
A Focus is a canonical subject within a Theme that describes a Realm more specifically.
Each Realm has one Primary Focus and may have up to two Supporting Focuses when they add meaningful context.
Example:
Theme: Place & Planet
Primary Focus: Energy, Infrastructure & Mobility
Supporting Focus: Environment, Climate & Conservation
Tag
A Tag is an open-ended descriptive label that adds specificity beyond the canonical Theme and Focus structure.
Tags can describe particular places, communities, movements, industries, technologies, practices, identities, subjects, or other relevant characteristics.
Example:
Tags: solar, renewable energy, West Virginia, installation
Classification Rule
Each Realm may have:
One Primary Theme
One Primary Focus within that Theme
Up to two Supporting Focuses from any Theme
Additional Tags as needed for specificity and discovery

4. Forces
Forces — the influences that shape behavior and movement
Forces are dynamic conditions that act upon Sources, Avatars, Allies, Realms, Elements, relationships, resources, and activity.
They influence what draws together or moves apart, what becomes visible or important, where attention, authority, energy, resources, and value flow, and how structures emerge, persist, compete, cooperate, weaken, renew, or change over time.
Unlike Elements, Forces are not things.
Unlike Actions, they are not discrete acts.
Unlike Capacities, they are not abilities.
Unlike Patterns, they are not reusable templates for accomplishing particular kinds of activity.
Forces describe what is acting upon the system.
Forces allow Kiduna to behave less like a collection of pages, feeds, menus, rankings, and notifications and more like a living world whose structure changes meaningfully according to who a Source is, how that Source is represented through an Avatar within a Realm, what matters to them, where they belong, what they are doing, what others are doing, and what is happening around them.
Forces can become part of both Kiduna’s semantic model and its interface physics.
Gravity need not merely be a metaphor. In Atlas, stronger relevance can literally exert greater visual Gravity.
Attention can bring something forward.
Affinity can form clusters and reveal meaningful proximity.
Exchange can become visible as flows.
Authority can reveal where legitimate power resides.
Influence can reveal where ideas or behavior are propagating.
Constraint can create boundaries.
Tension can become perceptible between incompatible or competing conditions.
Momentum can reveal accelerating activity.
Entropy can manifest as weakening or fading structures.
Regeneration can visibly restore vitality.
Forces may interact, reinforce one another, oppose one another, or change in strength over time.
The state of a Realm, Scene, relationship, Source, or Avatar is therefore not merely the result of what Elements exist within it, but also of the Forces currently acting upon them.
First-Principles Distinction
Elements are what exists.
Relationships describe how things are connected.
Forces describe what acts upon them.
Actions are what happens.
Capacities describe what they are able to do.

Gravity — attraction and relevance
Gravity determines how Realms, Elements, Avatars, Actors, Media, Resources, Actions, Offers, relationships, knowledge, and activity are organized in relation to a Source.
Everything in Kiduna may exert different Gravity for different Sources and at different moments.
Gravity can reflect signals such as:
Themes, Focuses, and Tags that matter to the Source;
Realms the Source belongs to, follows, visits, or contributes to;
Roles the Source holds within those Realms;
relationships with other Sources and Allies;
relationships among their Avatars within particular Realms;
current Projects, Programs, responsibilities, and commitments;
recent or continuing activity;
Actions awaiting the Source;
questions, requests, Offers, or opportunities relevant to them;
governance proposals, policies, deliberations, and votes;
deadlines, events, changes, or emerging conditions;
the Source’s current Scene, Realm, Surface, activity, or intent;
patterns of meaningful participation over time.
Gravity is related to what conventional systems call search, ranking, recommendations, notifications, activity feeds, prioritization, and navigation, but its purpose is fundamentally different.
Its purpose is not to capture the Source’s attention for someone else.
Its purpose is to help the Source orient within the richest possible world.
Something with strong Gravity may move closer, become more visible, become easier to encounter, appear more prominently in a Viewport, or be brought into conversation by Ki because it is meaningfully relevant in the present context.
Something with weak Gravity does not cease to exist. It simply recedes until its relationship to the Source becomes more relevant again.
Gravity can change continuously as the Source, the world, and the relationships between them change.
Gravity asks: What matters here, to this Source, now?

Affinity — likeness and belonging
Affinity is the Force that expresses meaningful attraction, compatibility, complementarity, and belonging among Sources, as expressed contextually through their relationships, activity, and Avatars within Realms.
Where Gravity organizes the broader world in relation to a Source, Affinity describes why another Source or Ally may feel near, relevant, familiar, complementary, or worth encountering.
Affinity can emerge through relationships such as:
Themes, Focuses, and Tags held in common;
shared Realms;
related Roles;
common Projects, Programs, Organizations, Communities, Councils, or Clans;
shared relationships with other Sources or Allies;
relationships among Avatars in particular Realms;
complementary Abilities, Wisdom, resources, needs, or interests;
common participation, contribution, creation, governance, or experience;
overlapping histories and pathways through Kiduna;
mutually relevant Offers or opportunities;
patterns of trust, reciprocity, collaboration, and interaction.
Affinity does not require similarity.
Two Sources may have strong Affinity because they share interests or experience, but they may also have strong Affinity because their differences are meaningfully complementary.
Affinity can help Sources understand not merely that another Source or Ally is relevant, but why they are drawn together.
Kiduna may make this relationship legible by showing the shared Realms, Themes, relationships, activities, histories, Roles, Avatars, or complementary qualities through which the Affinity arises.
Affinity is contextual rather than universal.
The same two Sources may have strong Affinity in one Realm, Theme, Project, or moment and little Affinity in another.
Affinity can influence discovery, introductions, collaboration, organization, and the spatial or visual proximity of Avatars, Sources, and Allies within Scenes and Viewports.
Affinity asks: Why are we drawn together?

Agency — intention and initiative
Agency is the Force generated when a Source, Ally, or collective chooses, initiates, creates, responds, or changes something.
Agency gives direction to possibility.
It appears when intention becomes consequential: when someone decides to act, proposes a new direction, starts a Realm, contributes work, creates something, organizes others, commits Resources, exercises a permission, delegates responsibility, or intentionally changes the state of the world.
Agency may be individual or collective.
Individual human Agency originates with Sources.
Within a Realm, a Source may express that Agency through an Avatar under the Source’s direct control.
The Avatar does not originate independent human authority. It represents and carries the Source’s identity, intention, choices, movement, and explicitly authorized Actions within that Realm.
Collective Agency emerges when Sources coordinate their intentions through relationships, governance, shared purpose, delegated authority, Actors, Allies, or Realms.
Allies and Actors may exercise operational agency within the authority, instructions, Resources, permissions, and boundaries provided to them, but human Sources remain the origin of legitimate human authority and consent.
Strong Agency can alter other Forces.
A Source may deliberately overcome weak Gravity by seeking something out.
A community may counter Entropy through organized activity.
A Realm may reduce Constraint by developing new Resources.
Collective Agency may transform Tension into a decision, project, agreement, or new Realm.
Agency therefore represents the capacity of intention to become movement.
Agency asks: What are we choosing to make happen?

Trust — confidence and reliance
Trust strengthens relationships and lowers the friction required for Sources, Avatars, Allies, Actors, and Realms to coordinate, exchange, delegate, share, collaborate, and act together.
Trust can arise through direct relationships, repeated interaction, demonstrated competence, fulfilled commitments, transparent behavior, shared history, institutional reputation, verifiable Records, governance, or other evidence.
Trust is contextual.
A Source may trust another Source as a Creator but not as a financial steward.
An Avatar may embody a trusted Source within one Realm while presenting a different contextual role in another.
A Realm may trust an Actor to perform a particular Action without granting it broader authority.
An Ally may be trusted within one Realm but unknown within another.
Trust is therefore not a single universal score.
It may exist differently across relationships, activities, Roles, Realms, and types of responsibility.
Trust may accumulate, weaken, recover, or disappear.
It can influence Affinity, Exchange, delegation, discovery, collaboration, and the amount of verification or oversight required before something can occur.
Trust never substitutes for explicit authority where authority is required.
Trust asks: What can reasonably be relied upon here?

Authority — legitimate power
Authority determines where recognized decision-making power resides and how strongly decisions, rules, permissions, mandates, or governance outcomes act upon the system.
Authority may arise through membership, governance, Roles, delegation, ownership, stewardship, contractual relationships, system rules, or other legitimate sources established within Kiduna or connected systems.
Authority is bounded.
It exists within a scope.
A Source may exercise Realm authority through their Avatar, but the Avatar does not independently own that authority. It expresses authority held or delegated to the Source in that Realm.
A Mage may possess administrative authority within one Realm but none in another.
A Council may have authority over a particular domain but not over unrelated activity.
An Actor may be authorized to execute an Action without possessing authority to redefine the policy governing that Action.
Authority is distinct from Influence.
Someone may possess significant Influence without formal Authority.
Someone may possess legitimate Authority while exerting little Influence.
Authority is also distinct from Agency.
Agency generates intentional action.
Authority determines whether a particular Source, Ally, Actor, or Realm possesses the recognized right to make or execute a consequential decision.
Authority asks: Who has the legitimate power to decide or act here?

Influence — the ability to affect others
Influence changes attention, understanding, preference, interpretation, behavior, or direction without necessarily exercising formal Authority.
Influence can arise through relationships, reputation, expertise, communication, example, creativity, persuasion, contribution, leadership, cultural significance, network position, or the circulation of ideas.
A Luminary may exert Influence through a body of work.
An Organizer may influence participation through relationships.
A Creator may influence understanding through Media.
An Ally may influence a Source by offering analysis or recommendations.
A Source may influence a Realm through participation expressed by their Avatar without possessing governance Authority.
Influence can propagate through relationships and networks.
It may strengthen Gravity around ideas, Sources, Realms, Media, or Actions.
It may create Momentum.
It may generate Affinity.
It may also create Tension when different forms of Influence pull participants in competing directions.
Influence should be legible where meaningful, but Kiduna does not treat Influence as permission or legitimacy.
Influence asks: What is affecting how people understand, choose, or act?

Attention — directed awareness
Attention determines what becomes foregrounded, discovered, considered, remembered, examined, and acted upon.
Attention is finite.
Many things may possess Gravity, but only some can occupy a Source’s immediate awareness at any given moment.
Attention therefore mediates between the richness of the world and the limits of human perception.
Attention may be directed intentionally by the Source or emerge through context.
A Source may explicitly focus on a Project.
An Avatar may face, approach, select, inspect, or interact with something in a Scene, expressing the Source’s Attention within that Realm.
An urgent governance Action may warrant temporary prominence.
A Source entering a particular Scene through their Avatar may naturally encounter the Resources or relationships most meaningful there.
Ki may surface something because current context makes it unusually relevant.
Attention can also be shared.
A Realm may collectively focus on an event, decision, crisis, creation, opportunity, or question.
Kiduna should distinguish between helping Attention find what matters and attempting to capture Attention for its own sake.
Attention is not an engagement metric to maximize.
It is a scarce form of human presence to respect.
Attention asks: What is being consciously encountered now?

Incentive — motivation through consequence
Incentive encourages or discourages behavior through rewards, costs, opportunities, recognition, obligations, access, reputation, compensation, or other consequences.
Incentives may be economic, social, cultural, organizational, experiential, or symbolic.
They may include:
financial compensation;
token rewards;
recognition;
access to Resources;
opportunities to participate;
reputation;
responsibility;
ownership;
privileges;
reciprocity;
social belonging;
penalties;
costs;
obligations;
or foregone opportunities.
Incentive does not imply manipulation.
Healthy Incentives make the consequences of choices legible and align participation with purposes Sources and Realms have consciously established.
Incentives can affect Agency, Exchange, Momentum, contribution, participation, and the allocation of Resources.
They can also create unintended behavior when poorly aligned.
For that reason, Incentives should remain inspectable and governable.
Incentive asks: What consequences make one course of action more or less attractive?

Exchange — reciprocal flow
Exchange moves value, Resources, knowledge, services, commitments, Media, access, labor, opportunities, and other forms of contribution among Sources, Allies, Actors, and Realms.
Exchange may be economic but is not limited to transactions.
A Source may exchange money for a service.
Two Sources may exchange knowledge.
Their Avatars may conduct, represent, negotiate, or experience that Exchange within a Realm.
A Realm may exchange access for contribution.
A Creator may contribute Media and receive recognition, ownership, compensation, or reciprocal assistance.
Communities may exchange care, labor, information, introductions, hospitality, or responsibility.
Exchange creates flows through the Kiduna world.
Those flows can form networks, strengthen relationships, create dependencies, generate Momentum, alter Incentives, build Trust, and distribute Resources.
Exchange can be immediate or extended across time.
Not every Exchange must be symmetrical in the moment, but reciprocal systems remain sustainable only when participants perceive the larger flow as legitimate and worthwhile.
Exchange asks: What is moving between us, and what is being given or received?

Constraint — limitation and boundary
Constraint restricts what can occur through rules, scarcity, permissions, dependencies, commitments, physical limits, technical limits, governance, safety requirements, privacy, time, available Resources, or other conditions.
Constraints are not inherently negative.
Some Constraints protect agency.
Privacy boundaries constrain access.
Governance rules constrain Authority.
Design systems constrain presentation enough to create coherence.
Resource limits create practical boundaries.
Physical space creates spatial limits.
Commitments constrain future choices because something has already been promised.
Constraints can reveal the true shape of possibility.
They can stimulate creativity, create safety, establish trust, or protect shared agreements.
Other Constraints may be unnecessary, outdated, unjust, or removable through Agency.
Kiduna should make meaningful Constraints intelligible rather than hiding them.
Constraint asks: What limits what can happen here?

Tension — competing pressures
Tension emerges when purposes, interests, values, needs, Resources, relationships, commitments, or Forces pull a system in different directions.
Tension does not necessarily represent conflict.
It may indicate a meaningful difference that requires interpretation, negotiation, governance, creativity, or choice.
Examples include:
individual interest and collective purpose;
privacy and transparency;
speed and deliberation;
scarcity and demand;
stability and experimentation;
local autonomy and ecosystem coherence;
different governance preferences;
competing uses of Resources;
contradictory Sources of Authority;
or incompatible desired outcomes.
Tension may weaken a system if ignored.
It may also create productive energy.
New ideas, agreements, institutions, Designs, Projects, and governance structures often emerge from Tension.
Making Tension perceptible allows participants to respond before disagreement becomes hidden dysfunction.
Tension asks: What is pulling in different directions?

Momentum — accumulated movement
Momentum causes established patterns of participation, activity, adoption, creation, organization, exchange, or change to become easier to continue.
Momentum emerges when activity compounds.
A Project with active contributors attracts further contribution.
A Realm with growing participation generates more opportunities for relationship.
A successful Exchange creates the conditions for further Exchange.
Repeated Actions may become practices.
Practices may become culture.
Culture may become institutional structure.
Momentum can therefore amplify Agency, Influence, Gravity, Affinity, Exchange, and Incentive.
Momentum is not inherently desirable.
A harmful or obsolete process can also develop Momentum.
Kiduna should make Momentum legible without treating acceleration itself as success.
Momentum matters because systems possess trajectories as well as states.
Momentum asks: What is already moving, and where is that movement carrying the system?

Entropy — dispersion and decay
Entropy causes relationships, knowledge, coordination, structures, Resources, practices, and activity to weaken, fragment, disappear, become outdated, or become disordered unless renewed.
Every living system experiences Entropy.
Relationships weaken without interaction.
Knowledge becomes stale.
Projects lose participants.
Resources are depleted.
Processes become disconnected from their original purpose.
Organizations accumulate outdated structures.
Scenes become cluttered.
Unused information becomes difficult to navigate.
Entropy is therefore not failure.
It is a natural Force that makes maintenance, renewal, stewardship, pruning, archiving, succession, and Regeneration necessary.
Kiduna may make Entropy perceptible through changes in activity, relationship strength, relevance, resource condition, participation, or other contextual signals.
Things affected by Entropy need not simply disappear.
They may become archival, dormant, historical, transformed, or available for renewal.
Entropy asks: What is weakening, dispersing, or losing coherence over time?

Regeneration — renewal and restoration
Regeneration restores depleted relationships, Resources, Capacities, ecosystems, communities, institutions, knowledge, structures, and activity and increases their ability to flourish.
Where Entropy describes natural dispersion and decay, Regeneration describes the processes through which living systems renew themselves.
Regeneration may occur through:
care;
maintenance;
repair;
reconciliation;
education;
new contribution;
new membership;
resource replenishment;
institutional renewal;
ecological restoration;
succession;
rediscovery of forgotten knowledge;
new relationships;
new forms of participation;
or deliberate redesign.
Regeneration is more than preservation.
It can leave a system more capable, resilient, diverse, connected, or generative than it was previously.
A Realm undergoing Regeneration may develop new Momentum.
Trust may be rebuilt.
Constraints may be reconsidered.
Resources may recover.
Relationships may reconnect.
New Agency may emerge from what had become dormant.
Regeneration asks: What is being restored, renewed, or made capable of flourishing again?

Force Interaction
Forces rarely act alone.
A Source may encounter a Realm because Gravity brings it close and Affinity reveals meaningful relationships within it.
The Source may then enter or participate in that Realm through an Avatar.
Trust may make Exchange easier.
Exchange may generate Incentive.
Incentive may increase Agency.
Agency may generate Momentum.
Momentum may increase Influence.
Authority may constrain what Agency can legitimately do.
Constraint may generate Tension.
Tension may lead to governance, creation, or reorganization.
Entropy may weaken Momentum.
Regeneration may restore it.
Attention mediates what becomes perceptible throughout this process.
Forces therefore form dynamic relationships rather than independent scores.
Kiduna may use them to understand, represent, explain, and organize changing conditions without reducing complex human relationships to a single metric.

Force Principle
Forces serve the agency of the Source.
They exist to make relationships, conditions, movement, and relevance intelligible; help Sources navigate complexity; and reveal meaningful possibilities within the world.
They do not exist to maximize engagement, manufacture urgency, manipulate behavior, create addictive loops, or sell access to Attention.
Forces may influence presentation, but they do not themselves create Authority.
Gravity cannot grant permission.
Affinity cannot create membership.
Trust cannot substitute for authorization.
Influence cannot override governance.
Attention does not create importance merely because something receives it.
Incentive does not nullify consent.
Momentum does not legitimize a direction simply because it is already moving.
No Force can override privacy, consent, governance, Roles, system-level boundaries, or legitimate Authority.
Forces shape how the world becomes perceptible and how conditions within it evolve.
The Source remains sovereign within it.

5. Realms
Realms are the living contexts inside Kiduna where Sources, represented through Avatars where appropriate, come together with Allies, resources, relationships, knowledge, and activity around something that matters.
Realms can stand alone as Surfaced Realms or exist within other Realms as Nested Realms. Each Realm can develop its own identity, Capacities, membership, governance, resources, and ways of acting.
Ecosystem — a connected whole
A broad network of Realms, Sources, Avatars, resources, and relationships that participate in a shared environment or purpose.
Organization — an enduring body
A member-governed entity formed to pursue a continuing purpose, coordinate activity, hold resources, and act collectively.
Alliance — a partnership
A Realm formed when Sources, organizations, or other Realms join around a shared objective while retaining their individual identities.
Program — a sustained initiative
An organized body of related activities, Projects, and resources directed toward an ongoing goal or outcome.
Project — a focused undertaking
A coordinated effort created to accomplish something specific, usually with defined objectives, participants, and a lifecycle.
Dyad — a shared connection
A Realm centered on an ongoing relationship between two and only two Sources, Allies, organizations, or other Realms, with its own context and history.
Community — a group that belongs together
A Realm of three or more Sources, Allies, Actors, organizations, or other Realms formed around shared identity, place, interest, experience, practice, or purpose where relationships and participation can grow over time.
Institution — an established function
A Realm created to perform a durable social, civic, cultural, educational, economic, or other recognized function.
Council — a body for deliberation and stewardship
A Realm formed to bring designated Sources or Allies together to advise, coordinate, deliberate, make recommendations, or exercise defined authority over a shared domain.
Concept — an idea made inhabitable
A Realm organized around a subject, question, body of knowledge, Theme, or idea so Sources and Allies can explore and develop it together.
Cell — a small autonomous unit
A lightweight Realm for a small group, function, team, or activity that can operate independently while remaining part of something larger.
Clan — a living chain of invitation and organization
A Realm formed by the Sources brought into Kiduna through a Source’s four generations of invitations.
A Clan makes the downline visible as a living network of human relationships, participation, organization, and contribution, giving each Source a context for supporting, connecting, and organizing the people whose path into Kiduna descends from their own.

6. Elements
Elements are the fundamental entities that exist and interact inside Kiduna.
Patterns arrange them, Forces influence how they relate and become perceptible, Surfaces present them, and Sources use Avatars and intelligence to create, organize, govern, exchange, and act.
Sources — humans
Sources are the human beings at the center of Kiduna.
A Source is a person—the ultimate origin of human identity, intention, authority, consent, ownership, responsibility, and agency.
The Source is not merely an account, profile, character, wallet, interface representation, or intelligent proxy.
A Source persists across Realms, Scenes, Modes, Surfaces, and representations.
The Source may be represented differently in different contexts, but the human remains the Source.
Human authority ultimately belongs to Sources. Other Elements may represent, assist, or act on behalf of a Source only within the permissions, delegation, governance, and constraints that make such action legitimate.
The Source is the human.
Avatars — representation
An Avatar is the representation of a Source within a Realm.
It is how the human becomes present, visible, situated, and directly interactive inside that Realm and its Scenes.
An Avatar is controlled by its Source.
It may express the Source’s:
identity;
appearance;
name or contextual presentation;
position;
movement;
gesture;
orientation;
Attention;
presence;
Roles;
permissions;
status;
relationships;
Actions;
and other Realm-specific characteristics.
An Avatar may be literal, symbolic, abstract, diagrammatic, spatial, or otherwise appropriate to the Scene and its Form, Dimension, Design, and Surface.
In a Literal Spatial Scene, an Avatar might appear as a person moving through a room.
In an isometric Scene, it might appear as an isometric figure.
In a Diagrammatic Scene, it might appear as a node, portrait, sigil, or other representation.
In AR, the Source may be represented through their physical presence, an overlay, or both.
In VR, the Avatar may provide embodied representation.
The same Source may therefore have different Avatar representations across different Realms, Scenes, Surfaces, or contexts without becoming a different Source.
An Avatar does not possess independent human identity, consent, ownership, or authority.
It carries and expresses those properties from the Source according to the Source’s Roles, permissions, choices, and the rules of the Realm.
Actions performed directly through an Avatar are Actions of the Source unless explicitly delegated or mediated through another Element.
The Source is the human. The Avatar is the human’s representative within a Realm.
Realms — contexts
The spaces where things belong and happen: Ecosystems, Organizations, Alliances, Programs, Projects, Dyads, Communities, Institutions, Councils, Concepts, Cells, Clans, and other nested or surfaced worlds.
Actors — operators
Specialized intelligent entities that perform defined roles and work within a Realm—such as Operators, Envoys, Sentinels, Researchers, Workers, and Supervisors.
Actors are distinct from Avatars.
An Avatar represents and is controlled by a human Source.
An Actor is an intelligent operational entity that performs work within its defined instructions, permissions, and authority.
Media — expression
The content Sources and Allies create, share, encounter, and experience: text, images, audio, video, documents, interfaces, and interactive works.
Allies — intelligence
Intelligent companions shaped by Sources or Realms to understand context, collaborate, advise, create, and act within defined permissions.
Allies are distinct from Avatars.
An Avatar represents a human Source.
An Ally is intelligence with which a Source or Realm relates and collaborates.
Resources — usable holdings and instruments
Resources are things and instruments that can be held, used, shared, allocated, presented, verified, redeemed, consumed, transferred, earned, or stewarded.
They carry utility, proof, access, benefit, opportunity, admission, or value.
The eight canonical Resource types are:
Capacity — spendable utility value — spend
Badge — verified accomplishment — present or verify
Code — access or activation — use or enter
Coupon — conditional benefit — redeem
Invitation — opportunity to participate — accept or decline
Ticket — admission or reserved participation — present or use
Gift — freely given value — give or receive
Reward — earned value or benefit — earn or claim
Resource types can share properties such as issuer, giver, holder, recipient, subject, scope, transferability, validity, expiration, remaining uses, earning criteria, claim status, revocation, provenance, and current state where relevant.
Capacity — prepaid utility credit
Capacity is the cryptocurrency token used as prepaid credit for intelligence, Compute, storage, and other services within Kiduna.
Capacity carries spendable utility value. It can be acquired, held, allocated, transferred where permitted, and spent as services and resources are used.
Capacity is an economic Resource. It is distinct from Capacities, which describe what a Realm, Ally, or Actor knows, can access, can do, and can remain coherent around.
Badges — verified accomplishment
Badges are verifiable credentials showing that a Source has completed, achieved, contributed to, qualified for, or been accredited in relation to something.
A Badge represents an attestation made by an identifiable issuer according to defined criteria. It may include evidence, an issue date, an expiration date, a level, a status, or other information needed to understand and verify the accomplishment.
Badges are normally bound to the Source that earned or received them and are not transferable.
A Badge may inform eligibility, recognition, access, or a Role, but it does not independently create authority or permission unless the rules of a Realm explicitly provide that effect.
Codes — access and activation
Codes are keys that unlock, activate, connect, admit, or authorize something within a defined scope.
A Code may provide entry to a Realm, activate a Resource or benefit, connect a relationship, unlock content or functionality, or authorize a bounded interaction.
Codes may be public or private, assigned or bearer-held, reusable or single-use, permanent or expiring, and transferable or non-transferable.
Kinship Codes are included within Codes.
A Code is the key or activation instrument. It is distinct from the access, relationship, Role, permission, or Action that results from using it.
Coupons — conditional benefit
Coupons are vouchers or entitlements that provide a discount, credit, benefit, access, or other advantage when redeemed under defined conditions.
A Coupon specifies what benefit it carries, who may redeem it, where it applies, when it remains valid, and whether it can be transferred or used more than once.
A Coupon may be represented or activated by a Code, but the Coupon is the benefit-bearing Resource while the Code is the mechanism used to access or activate it.
A Coupon may support an Offer or Exchange, but it is not itself the Offer or the completed Exchange.
Invitations — opportunity to participate
Invitations are Resources that give a Source or eligible holder the opportunity to enter, join, attend, connect, contribute, or participate.
An Invitation may provide an opportunity to enter a Realm, join a Project or Community, attend an event, establish a relationship, participate in an activity, or respond to another defined opening.
Invitations may be personal or shareable, open or restricted, transferable or non-transferable, reusable or single-use, revocable, and time-limited.
An Invitation can be delivered or activated through a Code, but the Invitation is the opportunity being extended while the Code is the key used to exercise it.
An Invitation can be accepted, declined, ignored, withdrawn, revoked, or allowed to expire. It does not create membership, relationship, or participation until it is accepted and any applicable conditions are satisfied.
Tickets — admission and reserved participation
Tickets are Resources that establish a holder’s entitlement to admission, attendance, transportation, service, an experience, or reserved participation.
A Ticket may provide general admission or identify a specific event, Realm, Scene, session, date, time, route, seat, place, quantity, service, or participant.
Tickets may be purchased, earned, granted, gifted, transferred, reserved, claimed, presented, validated, used, refunded, revoked, or allowed to expire according to their governing terms.
A Ticket may be transferable or bound to a particular Source, single-use or reusable, free or paid, physical or digital, and open-ended or valid only for a defined time and place.
A Ticket may contain or be represented by a Code, but the Ticket is the admission-bearing Resource while the Code is the key or validation mechanism.
A Ticket is distinct from an Invitation. An Invitation extends an opportunity to participate and may be accepted or declined. A Ticket confirms an entitlement or reservation to participate, subject to its stated conditions.
A Ticket is distinct from a Coupon. A Ticket provides admission or participation; a Coupon modifies the price, terms, or benefits associated with obtaining or using something.
Gifts — freely given value
Gifts are Resources voluntarily offered or transferred to another Source, Realm, or eligible recipient without requiring reciprocal payment or exchange.
A Gift may carry Capacity, money, access, Media, property, tools, data, time, services, or another form of value that can legitimately be given.
Gifts may be personal or collective, tangible or digital, immediate or scheduled, unrestricted or purpose-bound, anonymous or attributed, and revocable before acceptance where the governing rules allow.
A Gift may be offered, accepted, declined, delivered, received, returned, or redirected.
A Gift is distinguished from an Exchange by the absence of required reciprocal value. If receipt depends on a required payment, trade, or contribution, the interaction is an Offer or Exchange rather than a Gift.
Rewards — earned value
Rewards are Resources earned by satisfying defined criteria, completing Actions, contributing, participating, reaching milestones, winning, or producing recognized outcomes.
A Reward may carry Capacity, money, access, a Coupon, a Gift, a Ticket, a privilege, or another benefit made available to the Source or participant who earned it.
Rewards should identify the issuer, earning criteria, eligible recipient, benefit, status, availability, claim requirements, expiration, and any conditions governing their use.
A Reward may be automatic or manually granted, immediate or delayed, claimed or delivered automatically, transferable or non-transferable, and revocable only according to legitimate and visible rules.
A Reward is distinct from a Badge. A Badge verifies or represents an accomplishment; a Reward provides value or benefit because an accomplishment or qualifying condition occurred. The same achievement may produce both.
A Reward is distinct from a Gift because it is contingent on something being earned. It is distinct from compensation when it is discretionary, supplemental, competitive, or milestone-based rather than payment owed for agreed work.
An Incentive is the Force created by an anticipated consequence. A Reward is the actual Resource earned or received when the relevant conditions are fulfilled.
Actions — execution
Defined things that can be done: operations that change state, invoke capabilities, move resources, create records, or produce effects inside Kiduna or connected systems.
Actions can operate upon Resources by spending Capacity, presenting or verifying Badges, using Codes, redeeming Coupons, accepting or declining Invitations, presenting or using Tickets, giving or receiving Gifts, and earning or claiming Rewards.
Offers — exchange
Proposals to provide, request, trade, contribute, or collaborate around something of value—connecting needs with available Sources, capabilities, resources, and opportunities.
An Offer may make a Gift, Reward, Coupon, Invitation, Ticket, or other Resource available, but each Resource becomes effective according to its own acceptance, earning, redemption, or use conditions.

7. Roles
Roles are Realm-scoped descriptions and grants that classify an interaction with a Realm and define how an identified Source participates, contributes, and—where explicitly authorized—acts within that Realm.
A Badge may inform eligibility for a Role, and other Resources may affect access or participation, but no Resource independently creates a Role or Authority unless the rules of the Realm explicitly provide that effect.
A Source holds the Role.
The Source’s Avatar expresses that Role within the Realm.
Roles do not belong independently to the Avatar, because the Avatar is a Realm representation of the Source rather than a separate holder of identity or authority.
Roles apply only to the Realm that grants them.
The Kiduna Ecosystem is the master Realm, but an Ecosystem Role is not a global Role.
A Source may have one Role in the Ecosystem and different Roles in other Realms.
Roles include Realm relationship Roles—Visitor, Guest, Member, and Mage—and contribution Roles—Catalyst, Organizer, Creator, Builder, and Luminary.
A Source may hold multiple Roles in the same Realm when that Realm allows it.
Realm Relationship Roles
Visitor — unidentified interaction
A Visitor is a human interacting without an account or without authenticated identity in the interaction.
This can happen through social media, Telegram, email, or another external channel, where an Ally or Actor may be interacting with someone or something that Kiduna cannot identify or verify as a Source.
Visitor is a classification of the interaction, not proof of identity.
It conveys no account-based or Realm membership privileges.
Guest — admitted, not a Member
A Guest is a Source who has an account and is allowed into a Realm but has not been granted the privileges of a Member of that Realm.
A Guest has only the access and privileges that the Realm explicitly grants to Guests.
The Guest may be represented within the Realm through an Avatar.
Member — full Member privileges
A Member is a Source who has all the privileges assigned to Members by that Realm.
Because Member privileges are defined independently by each Realm, being a Member in one Realm does not make the Source a Member in any other Realm.
The Source’s Avatar expresses those Member privileges when the Source participates within the Realm.
Mage — bounded Realm administration
A Mage is a Source granted the Realm-scoped equivalent of administrator, owner, or super-administrator authority.
A Mage may perform any administrative task that the Realm authorizes and that the system itself does not prohibit.
The Mage may exercise those capabilities through their Avatar or other authorized interfaces.
Mages may assign and remove privileges from Sources within their Realm.
They may also take specifically authorized protective actions, such as changing offensive public Media to Personal so that only the Source who posted it can see it.
Mage authority never overrides system-level privacy, safety, security, or access limits.
A Mage cannot view personal information, use Mage status in one Realm to enter another private Realm, or exercise Mage powers outside the particular Realm in which the Role was granted.
Contribution Roles
01 — Catalyst
Bring a project, program, or organization into being.
Catalysts clarify the initial purpose, gather the founding participants, assemble the necessary resources, and carry an emerging idea through formation and into motion.
Start a movement that matters.
02 — Organizer
Form and sustain the relationships that make collective action possible.
Organizers bring people together around a shared purpose, help them find their place, and nurture the participation and activity that grow through the communities they support.
Build a community with purpose and power.
03 — Creator
Turn knowledge, imagination, and lived experience into living capabilities.
Creators add Wisdom, shape an organization’s Presence, design its stories and experiences, train its Allies, and create the instructions, Media, methods, and processes through which its purpose becomes real.
Bring the work to life.
04 — Builder
Create the apps, agents, tools, automations, systems, and infrastructure that expand what an organization can do.
Builders make valuable work easier to perform, repeat, improve, share, and extend into entirely new forms of individual and collective agency.
Expand the realm of possibility.
05 — Luminary
Contribute a defining work, body of wisdom, creative practice, or deep intellectual grounding around which a collective can form and grow.
Luminaries may be supported through project budgets, royalties, repayment of development costs, or allocations established by the organization.
Give the work depth and direction.
Realm-Defined Rights
Every Role receives its actual rights within the Realm that grants it; the Role name alone does not create a universal permission set.
For example, a Source granted the Creator Role receives the rights the Realm’s Catalyst or Catalysts have defined for Creator in that Realm, subject to the Realm’s current governance and immutable system limits.
The Source may exercise those rights through their Avatar or other authorized interfaces.
Authority is established through Governance.
Permissions determine which Actions may be taken.
Roles identify the Realm-scoped relationship, contribution, or entrusted authority to which those permissions apply.
Membership Authority
Any Realm rule, guardrail, setting, or policy—including Role definitions and privilege assignments—may be changed by the membership through whatever process the membership has established.
Membership governance cannot override system-level limits, including the privacy and access boundaries that constrain Mages.
Role Principle
Roles are local to Realms, not global to Kiduna.
The Ecosystem is the master Realm, yet each other Realm defines and grants its own Roles independently.
A Source may:
hold more than one Role in the same Realm when that Realm allows it;
hold different Roles across different Realms;
be represented by different Avatars across different Realms or Scenes while remaining the same Source;
be a Member of the Ecosystem and a Guest in one Realm, a Creator in another, and a Mage in another;
contribute without holding a formal office;
move between Roles as participation changes;
be recognized and compensated for attributable contribution associated with one or more Roles.
Roles create a shared language for Realm-specific participation, contribution, and bounded authority without creating a global hierarchy.

8. Capacities
Capacities are the six foundational capabilities that shape what a Realm or Ally knows, how it behaves, what it can access, what it can do, and how it remains coherent.
Wisdom — inform
The knowledge and memory available to the Realm or Ally, typically held in a vector database and expanded through relevant information.
Presence — instruct
The system prompt and governing instructions that shape how the Realm or Ally presents itself, responds, reasons, and behaves in context.
Connections — empower
Integrations with third-party systems, software, services, data sources, communication tools, financial rails, and other external capabilities.
Automations — enable
Deep agents, triggers, workflows, and sequences that allow activity to begin, continue, and complete automatically.
Abilities — impart
Reusable skills, procedures, and markdown-based instructions that teach a Realm or Ally how to perform particular kinds of work.
Coherence — align
Sentinels, principles, constraints, guardrails, and shared values that help keep behavior, decisions, and activity aligned with the purpose and standards of the Realm.

9. Patterns
Patterns are reusable system templates inside Kiduna.
Each Pattern defines the basic structure, behaviors, relationships, and tools needed for a particular kind of activity, providing a proven starting point that can be adapted rather than built from scratch.
Viewport — navigate
See the larger landscape: Realms, Sources, Avatars, relationships, activity, and pathways between them.
Commons — organize
Gather Sources, represented through Avatars where appropriate, together with Allies; form relationships; coordinate participation; and organize around shared purpose.
Studio — create
Shape ideas, Media, experiences, Realms, Allies, and other things that can be created together.
Factory — build
Produce working systems: Actors, Automations, applications, integrations, tools, and infrastructure.
Garden — enjoy
Experience, explore, play, learn, gather, and interact with what Sources and Allies have created.
World — act
Operate in a living environment where Sources, acting through their Avatars, and intelligent entities can perform work, coordinate activity, and make things happen.
Forum — govern
Deliberate, propose, decide, establish policy, delegate authority, and govern shared Resources and Actions.
Exchange — settle
Make Offers, transact, exchange value, spend Capacity, redeem Coupons, purchase or transfer Tickets, give Gifts, distribute or claim Rewards, compensate contributions, and settle economic activity.
Vault — store
Hold and manage assets; Resources including Capacity, Badges, Codes, Coupons, Invitations, Tickets, Gifts, and Rewards; records; credentials; balances; and other things entrusted to a Source or Realm.

10. Modes
Modes are concurrent ways of presenting and working with the same underlying Realm or Scene.
They are not separate environments: the same Scene can be presented in Forge mode for one Source, Rehearse mode for another, and Field mode for another at the same time.
Mode is contextual to the Source and interaction.
One Source can be Forging while another is Fielding in the same Scene.
Where a Scene includes embodied, spatial, symbolic, or otherwise represented participation, the Source interacts through an Avatar appropriate to that context.
Forge — make changes
Forge is the mode for making changes.
Sources can design, assemble, configure, test, and evolve Realms, Allies, Actors, Avatars and their representations, complete Scenes, and their Patterns, Elements, Capacities, Forms, Components, Dimensions, and Designs.
Rehearse — validate a Draft
Rehearse is the mode for reviewing, testing, and experiencing a Draft as it is intended to work before it becomes Final in Field.
A Source may Rehearse through their Avatar exactly as they would participate in the Final Scene.
Publishing from Forge as Draft moves the relevant Realm, Ally, Actor, complete Scene, or other publishable work into Rehearse.
Field — participate and act
Field is the mode for participating in and acting within the Final published experience.
Sources participate through their Avatars where the Realm or Scene uses representation.
Sources, Allies, and Actors can organize, create, build, govern, settle, enjoy, and act according to their Roles and permissions.
Publishing from Rehearse as Final moves the relevant Realm, Ally, Actor, complete Scene, or other publishable work into Field.
Publishing lifecycle
Forge → publish as Draft → Rehearse → publish as Final → Field.
Publishing changes availability; it does not create a different Scene.
Draft and Final are publication states of the same underlying work, allowing further Forging while others continue Fielding the current Final.

11. Scenes
Scenes are situated compositions through which a Realm becomes present, experiential, and interactive.
A Scene brings together Elements, Capacities, Patterns, Forms, Components, Dimensions, Designs, and the effects of Forces within a particular context.
Sources experience and participate in Scenes.
Where representation is appropriate, they become present within a Scene through their Avatars.
A Scene can be presented concurrently in Forge, Rehearse, and Field according to each Source’s context, Role, permissions, and the Scene’s Draft or Final publication state.
Scenes move from Forge through Rehearse to Field by being published first as Draft and then as Final.
Because Forces are Source-relative and context-sensitive, the same underlying Scene may organize itself differently around different Sources without becoming a different Scene.
The Avatar provides the Scene-level representation through which those Source-relative conditions can become visible and interactive.
Gravity may alter prominence, proximity, pathways, or emphasis around a Source and their Avatar.
Affinity may make meaningful relationships among Sources, Avatars, and Allies visible.
Attention may be expressed through the Avatar’s orientation, selection, movement, or interaction and may bring particular Elements or activity into the foreground.
Exchange may appear as flows among participants or Realms.
Authority may reveal where decisions and permissions reside.
Tension may reveal competing pressures.
Momentum may make active trajectories perceptible.
Entropy and Regeneration may reveal where living systems are weakening or renewing.
A Realm may contain one Scene or many Scenes, each designed for a different purpose, activity, audience, or experience.

12. Forms — Types of Scenes
Forms define the primary visual and interaction grammar of a Scene.
Literal Spatial
Rooms, terrain, Objects, routes, Avatars, and embodied memory.
Symbolic
Emblems, talismans, connectors, Avatars expressed as symbols, and stable meaning.
Impressionistic
Fields, light, texture, rhythm, atmosphere, and sparse anchors.
Diagrammatic
Constellations, flows, dependencies, relationships, systems, governance, and Avatars represented as nodes or other diagrammatic forms.
Hybrid
Two or more Forms may coexist when one clearly leads and the combination serves the purpose of the Scene.

13. Components
Components are the reusable visual and interactive building blocks used to construct Scenes inside Kiduna.
They define the world, the things that inhabit it, the activity occurring within it, and the interfaces through which Sources interact with it.
An Avatar is an Element, not merely a Component.
Its visible or interactive representation may, however, be rendered through one or more Components according to the Scene’s Form, Dimension, Design, and Surface.
Backgrounds — environment
The world behind and around the grid: landscapes, skies, distant architecture, atmosphere, and other environmental context.
Tiles — ground
Traversable floor and terrain surfaces that establish where movement and placement can occur.
Walls — boundaries
Vertical partitions and barriers that define rooms, edges, enclosures, and divisions within a Scene.
Objects — forms
Static placed elements such as furniture, structures, equipment, vegetation, decorations, and other non-animated things in the world.
Entities — presences
Animated, addressable beings or active elements that can move, respond, communicate, or be interacted with.
Avatars, Allies, and Actors may each be visually expressed through Entity Components without becoming the same kind of Element.
Effects — activity
Transient visual or environmental phenomena such as light, weather, particles, energy, smoke, animation, and other momentary changes.
Portals — transitions
Entrances and spatial connections that move a Source and their Avatar between locations, Scenes, Realms, or other contexts.
Panels — embedded interfaces
Interface surfaces that exist inside the world—signs, consoles, displays, kiosks, dashboards, control surfaces, and floating interfaces.
Chrome — application interface
Interface surfaces that belong to the surrounding Kiduna experience rather than the world itself—toolbars, inspectors, inventories, navigation, controls, and status displays.
Key distinction: Panels are part of the Scene. Chrome is how a Source operates the Scene.

14. Dimensions
Dimensions define the spatial representation, scale, coordinates, and relationships through which Scenes and Components exist.
They make it possible for the same underlying Element, Component, or Scene to be expressed through different visual environments without becoming a completely different thing.
An Avatar, Object, Entity, Realm, Portal, interface, or other Component representation can therefore possess a consistent identity and relational scale even when represented differently across Desktop, Mobile, CTV, AR, VR, MR, XR, or future Surfaces.
Dimensions separate what something is from how it is spatially presented.
2D — planar
A flat coordinate space organized through horizontal and vertical position.
Used for interfaces, diagrams, maps, cards, documents, dashboards, symbolic compositions, Avatars, and other primarily planar experiences.
Components can be represented as vectors, raster images, text, interface objects, diagrams, or other two-dimensional forms.
2.5D — isometric
A spatially suggestive coordinate system that represents depth while remaining fundamentally composed and rendered as a two-dimensional Scene.
Used for isometric environments, layered worlds, rooms, landscapes, maps, organizational spaces, Avatars, and other Scenes where spatial relationships matter without requiring a complete three-dimensional simulation.
2.5D allows Components and Avatar representations to preserve depth, orientation, layering, adjacency, and relative scale while retaining the clarity, efficiency, and composability of two-dimensional assets.
3D — spatial
A three-dimensional coordinate space in which Components possess position, scale, orientation, volume, depth, and spatial relationships.
Used for immersive worlds, spatial interfaces, simulations, augmented environments, virtual environments, embodied Avatars, and other Scenes in which Sources and intelligent entities can move through or around space.
Scale
Dimensions also establish a shared system of scale.
Every spatial Component can describe its size and relationship to other Components using canonical measurements or proportional relationships rather than relying solely on the dimensions of a particular image, model, or asset.
This allows Components created by different Sources, systems, tools, and applications to work together predictably.
A door should relate sensibly to an Avatar.
An Avatar should relate sensibly to a building.
A building should relate sensibly to a landscape.
The representation may change.
The relationship does not.
Spatial Relationships
Dimensional metadata can describe relationships including:
position;
size;
scale;
orientation;
depth;
elevation;
bounds;
anchors;
attachment points;
adjacency;
containment;
traversal;
collision;
visibility;
distance;
perspective.
Not every Component needs every property.
The dimensional system provides a common language when those properties matter.
Cross-Dimensional Representation
A Component or Avatar may possess representations for one or more Dimensions.
For example, the same Avatar might have:
a 2D portrait or sigil;
a 2D presentation asset;
a 2.5D isometric representation;
a 3D embodied representation.
These are representations of the same Avatar and therefore ultimately of the same Source rather than unrelated identities.
Likewise, the same Object might have:
a 2D icon;
a 2D presentation asset;
a 2.5D isometric asset;
a 3D model.
These are representations of the same Component rather than unrelated assets.
Where an exact representation does not exist, Kiduna may adapt, project, simplify, generate, or substitute an appropriate representation while preserving the Component or Avatar’s identity and important relationships.
A Scene itself can also have more than one dimensional representation.
A Scene created as an isometric environment, for example, might be presented as a simplified map on Mobile and as an immersive spatial environment in VR.
Dimensional Principle
Identity persists across representation.
The Source remains the same human.
The Avatar remains the Source’s Realm representation.
Its appearance may change across Dimensions without changing who it represents.
Dimensions allow Scenes, Avatars, and Components to move between flat, isometric, and spatial experiences while preserving their underlying Realm, Elements, relationships, Actions, and meaning.
This makes Kiduna extensible to new interfaces and technologies without requiring the underlying system to be rebuilt around each new display medium.

15. Designs
Designs are reusable visual systems that determine how Scenes and Components look and feel.
A Design can establish the colors, typography, materials, shapes, spacing, iconography, lighting, motion, and other presentation rules through which a Realm or Scene expresses its identity.
Designs make visual expression portable, composable, and compatible rather than embedding appearance independently into every Component.
Avatar representations may also inherit or adapt to Design rules while preserving the identity of the Source they represent.
Colors
Palettes, semantic colors, states, contrast relationships, gradients, environmental colors, and other rules governing the use of color.
Typography
Font families, weights, scales, hierarchy, spacing, treatments, and other rules governing written language.
Shape
Corner systems, geometry, outlines, borders, depth, framing, proportions, and recurring visual forms.
Materials
Surface qualities such as glass, enamel, metal, paper, stone, fabric, wood, light, ceramics, plastics, natural materials, and other physical or digital treatments.
Iconography
Sigils, symbols, icons, pictograms, marks, and the visual rules that allow them to work coherently together.
Spacing
Grids, rhythm, margins, padding, density, scale, alignment, and proportional relationships.
Motion
Transitions, easing, movement character, duration, timing, responsiveness, and other animation conventions.
Lighting
Illumination, shadow, reflection, glow, atmosphere, environmental light, and other treatments relevant to spatial or rendered experiences.
Interface Treatment
The visual and interactive rules governing Panels, Chrome, controls, states, selections, overlays, notifications, feedback, and other interface behaviors.
Asset Treatment
Rules governing how Backgrounds, Tiles, Walls, Objects, Entities, Effects, Portals, Panels, Avatars, and other Components or representations appear together.
Design Systems
A Design may range from a small collection of presentation choices to a complete design system.
A full Design System can contain:
design tokens;
palettes;
typography;
icons and sigils;
reusable Components;
spacing and sizing systems;
shape systems;
materials;
interaction states;
accessibility requirements;
motion rules;
lighting rules;
dimensional variants;
implementation guidance.
Compatibility
Designs provide a compatibility layer between Components created by different Sources.
A Component does not have to be permanently bound to the exact colors, typography, material, or styling with which it was originally created.
Where appropriate, Components can expose designable properties that allow the active Design to determine their presentation.
Avatar representations may similarly adapt to the active Design where appropriate, while preserving characteristics necessary to maintain the continuity, legibility, and identity of the Source they represent.
This allows something created in one Realm to be brought into another Realm and adapted without losing either its identity or the coherence of its new environment.
Inheritance
Designs can be inherited.
A Realm may establish a Design.
A Nested Realm may inherit the Design of its parent.
A Scene may inherit the Design of its Realm.
A Scene may extend or override parts of that Design.
Individual Components may inherit the Scene Design while retaining specific characteristics that should not be overridden.
Avatar representations may inherit the Scene Design while retaining Source-specific or Realm-specific identity characteristics.
This creates a cascading visual system in which consistency is the default while meaningful difference remains possible.
Design + Dimension
Design and Dimension are independent but complementary.
Dimension determines spatial representation.
Design determines visual expression.
The same Design may therefore be expressed in 2D, 2.5D, and 3D.
Likewise, the same spatial Scene can be expressed through different Designs without changing the underlying Elements, relationships, geometry, or Actions.
An Avatar may similarly change visual treatment or dimensional representation while continuing to represent the same Source.
This separation allows Scenes to adapt simultaneously to different brands, communities, cultures, accessibility needs, devices, and spatial environments.
Design Principle
Structure should survive style, and identity should survive adaptation.
Designs allow Kiduna to support an expanding ecosystem of Sources, creators, Realms, Components, interfaces, Avatars, and Surfaces without forcing everything to look identical or allowing everything to become visually incompatible.

16. Surfaces
Surfaces are the device and experiential contexts through which Sources experience Kiduna.
The same Realms, Sources, Avatars, Allies, Actors, identity, relationships, Patterns, Scenes, Resources, Forces, and activity persist across Surfaces, but each Surface presents them in the form best suited to its capabilities, context, and mode of interaction.
A Surface does not define a separate Kiduna.
It provides another way of encountering the same underlying world.
A Source remains the same human across every Surface.
The Source’s Avatar may be represented differently according to the needs and capabilities of each Surface.
Desktop — create and coordinate
The full working Surface.
Best for sustained attention, complex creation, building, organizing, governance, working across Forge, Rehearse, and Field modes, and managing multiple parts of a Realm at once.
Desktop can support 2D, 2.5D, and 3D Scenes depending on their purpose.
A Source’s Avatar may appear as a portrait, node, isometric figure, spatial entity, or other Scene-appropriate representation.
Mobile — participate and act
The always-with-you Surface.
Best for conversation, notifications, approvals, identity, quick Actions, capturing information, navigating nearby context, and participating wherever you are.
Mobile may also serve as a controller, camera, sensor, identity device, or companion to other Surfaces.
A Source may control an Avatar appearing on another Surface from Mobile.
CTV — gather and experience
The shared-room Surface.
Best for communal experiences, presentations, live activity, games, Media, gatherings, shared experiences, and interacting with Kiduna together on a large screen.
Avatars may allow multiple Sources to become individually present within a shared CTV experience while controlling their participation through companion devices.
Chrome Extension — connect the web
The contextual Surface.
Brings Kiduna into the existing web so Sources can recognize Realms, Resources, relationships, and relevant context; work with what is on the page; and act without leaving the current environment.
Avatar representation may be minimal or symbolic where an embodied representation would add no value.
AR — augment
The augmented-reality Surface.
Places Kiduna Elements, information, interfaces, Allies, Actions, and Scenes into a Source’s view of the physical world.
AR allows digital context to become spatially associated with people, places, Objects, environments, and activity without replacing the surrounding physical environment.
A Realm might appear around a physical location.
An Object might reveal information or Actions when encountered.
An Ally might become spatially present beside a Source.
A Scene might overlay a workplace, neighborhood, museum, landscape, meeting, or event.
Because the Source’s physical body may already be perceptible in AR, an Avatar may appear as an identity layer, visual augmentation, spatial indicator, symbolic representation, or other contextual extension rather than as a duplicate body.
VR — immerse
The virtual-reality Surface.
Places Sources inside fully spatial Kiduna Scenes where environments, Objects, Entities, Avatars, Allies, interfaces, relationships, and activity can be experienced through embodied presence.
In VR, the Avatar becomes the Source’s primary embodied representation within the virtual Realm.
The Source controls its movement, orientation, gestures, interactions, and Actions.
VR is particularly suited to immersive Gardens, collaborative spaces, simulations, learning environments, gatherings, creation, exploration, storytelling, and spatial forms of work.
MR — integrate
The mixed-reality Surface.
Allows physical and digital space to operate as one interactive environment.
Kiduna Scenes and Components can understand and respond to the geometry, Objects, surfaces, movement, and other characteristics of a Source’s physical environment, allowing digital Elements to participate meaningfully within it.
A physical table might become part of a Forum.
A wall could become a shared Panel.
A digital Object could remain anchored to a physical location.
Sources in the same room could interact with shared Elements that occupy the space between them.
Avatars may combine physical presence with digital identity, Roles, state, Effects, or other Realm-specific representation.
XR — extend
The extended-reality Surface.
XR provides the generalized spatial-computing context spanning AR, VR, MR, and emerging forms that combine or move between them.
It allows Kiduna to support devices and experiences whose relationship between physical and digital space may change dynamically rather than fitting permanently into a single category.
XR also provides a category for future spatial interfaces that cannot yet be cleanly described as AR, VR, or MR.
An Avatar may correspondingly move between symbolic, augmented, and embodied representation while remaining bound to the same Source.
Surface Adaptation
Scenes do not need to look or behave identically across Surfaces.
A Realm might appear as:
a diagram or constellation in 2D;
an isometric environment on Desktop;
a compact contextual interface on Mobile;
a shared experience on CTV;
spatial information, Avatars, and Objects overlaid on the physical world in AR;
an inhabitable environment with embodied Avatars in VR;
an environment integrating physical Sources and digital Avatar representations in MR;
an experience that moves fluidly between physical and digital presence in XR.
These are different presentations of the same underlying Realm and Scene.
The Source remains the same human throughout.

Surface Principle
The world persists. The presentation adapts.
The Source persists. The Avatar represents.
A Source should be able to move between Realms, Scenes, Modes, and Surfaces without losing human identity, context, relationships, Resources, history, authority, or agency.
An Avatar may change form as the context changes, but the human it represents does not.
