Kiduna: Massively-multiplayer AI

A new architecture for the agentic internet.

David Levine · Catalyst, Kinship Duna · david@kiduna.ai

Abstract

Agency is the capacity to sense, choose, act, and learn. Individual agency allows a person to author their own life. Collective agency allows people to accomplish together what none could accomplish alone. The question running through human history is how to increase the second while preserving and expanding the first.

This paper follows that history through four ages: Kinship, organized around Relatedness; Feudal, organized around Control; Commercial, organized around Exchange; and Agentic, organized around Coordination. Each opens a new range of collective possibility. Each also changes where power lives and whose purposes the collective serves.

The ages accumulate. Relatedness remains alive in families, friendships, communities, and the informal relationships that make formal organizations work. Control systems divide and administer the larger world around them. Commercial flows connect people across those divisions, but nations, states, institutions, financial intermediaries, and platforms still determine much of who may exchange, on what terms, and through whose gates.

The Agentic Age can interweave with all three. Intelligence accountable to people and their collectives can help them find one another, understand their circumstances, negotiate commitments, coordinate resources, and act across institutional boundaries. Control structures can become more accountable to the relationships they govern. Commercial flows can sustain the people and purposes that create their value. Relatedness can become an organizing force at scales it could not previously reach.

Kiduna is a sociotechnical architecture for that possibility. It joins intelligent Allies, member governance, shared resources, privacy, and accountable action within a connected ecology of people and Realms. Its purpose is to make relatedness capable of scale: allowing people to coordinate freely and powerfully while remaining whole participants in the relationships they create.

I.

Agency all the way down

Life happens in relationship.

A cell maintains a boundary, responds to its surroundings, and participates in processes larger than itself. An organism coordinates many such processes. Living communities emerge through countless interactions among organisms and the environments they inhabit. At each scale, something persists through its capacity to respond.

Human agency gives that pattern a distinctive expression. We perceive a situation, imagine possibilities, choose among them, act, and learn from what happens. We also ask what matters, whom we care about, what we owe one another, and what kind of world we want to inhabit.

Relatedness gives those questions their living context. A person is somebody's friend, child, neighbor, collaborator, teacher, or beloved. A river sustains a place. A shared practice carries memory between generations. What we sense and choose is shaped by these relationships, and our actions change the conditions within which others live.

Collective agency emerges when those distinct capacities become capable of acting together. A group can restore a building, produce a film, care for a watershed, or establish a school. Its members gain possibilities that depend on one another. The quality of the collective depends on what happens to their agency as that shared capacity grows.

Institutions make this possible in many indispensable ways. They preserve knowledge, maintain infrastructure, establish rights, and carry responsibilities beyond a single lifetime. Yet scale also creates distance. A person becomes a case, a customer, an employee number, a voter, or a line in a budget. What cannot fit the system's categories becomes difficult for the system to see.

Kiduna begins with a different design requirement: a larger whole should increase what its participants can perceive, decide, accomplish, and understand. Their purposes, boundaries, and relationships must remain present as coordination grows more complex.

The collective becomes more capable because the people within it become more capable.

II.

Four ages.

Four living layers.

Human history can be read through the changing ways people organize their lives together. Who belongs? Who commands? Who may exchange? Who can coordinate with whom?

Kinship, Feudal, Commercial, and Agentic name four organizing logics. They describe historical changes in prominence and structures that coexist in the present. Relatedness, control, exchange, and coordination occur in every age; the distinction is which principle most strongly organizes the wider system.

The dates below are approximate landmarks in an interpretive history, not universal boundaries. Different societies followed different paths. “Feudal” is used here for the wider logic of control over access and obligation; medieval European feudalism is one historically specific expression of it. The emergence of our species does not establish a date for any particular kinship custom.

The Kinship Age

Relatedness · Human prehistory to the present

Beginnings: deep human prehistory, with the roughly 300,000-year history of Homo sapiens as our horizon. Transition: farming and settlement from about 10,000 BCE begin a long, uneven movement toward territorial administration; early states make that movement more pronounced by about 3500–3000 BCE. [1] [2]

Kinship organizes life through relatedness. People belong through family, place, shared practice, mutual obligation, and the memory of what they have lived through together. Knowledge travels through participation: showing, telling, making, witnessing, remembering.

In this logic, an obligation has a face. Someone knows who needs food, who has a skill, who has kept a promise, and who needs help. Decisions are situated within a web of relationships that gives them meaning. Giving, receiving, caring, teaching, and working form a continuous life.

Kinship can also enclose and constrain. Familiarity does not guarantee equality, freedom, or welcome to outsiders. The inheritance worth carrying forward is the capacity to recognize one another as whole participants, to hold obligations in context, and to care about consequences because they reach people and places we know.

That capacity remains everywhere around us. A family caring for an elder, neighbors repairing a home, friends beginning a venture, or colleagues quietly helping one another across departments are practicing relatedness. In much of modern life, it survives in pockets separated by organizational boundaries, property lines, schedules, jurisdictions, and systems that assign people different identities for different transactions.

We have extraordinary relational capacity. We have built a world in which much of it struggles to travel.

The Feudal Age

Control · Access governed from above

Beginnings: the wider control layer takes durable administrative form in early states around 3500–3000 BCE; recognizably feudal arrangements develop in parts of medieval Europe around the ninth to eleventh centuries CE. Transition: expanding towns and trade networks begin shifting the balance toward commerce around 1000–1500 CE, while control structures continue to evolve. [2] [3] [4]

The decisive change is the ability to organize people through control of something they need: land, protection, food stores, legal standing, a trade route, an office, or permission to participate. Authority attaches to a position within a structure. Access is granted on terms established by whoever governs the gate.

Medieval feudalism makes this logic vivid through landholding, service, rank, and obligation. Earlier states and later institutions differ greatly in their laws and legitimacy, yet they can share the underlying arrangement: collective life passes through authorities whose decisions reach beyond the relationships in which people directly know one another.

Control enables continuity, defense, administration, and large undertakings. It also separates relatedness into governable units. People become subjects of a territory, occupants of a property, members of a rank, or holders of an authorized status. The structure determines which relationships count and how obligations will be enforced.

This layer remains active in nations, states, institutions, corporate hierarchies, and privately governed networks. A passport, professional license, payment account, platform account, or procurement approval can determine whether two willing parties may work together. These arrangements are not all feudal institutions in the historical sense. They carry recognizable questions of control: who owns access, who sets the terms, and who can refuse or revoke participation?

Some gates protect people from violence, fraud, exploitation, or environmental harm. Others preserve privilege or make participation unnecessarily dependent on a gatekeeper. An agentic transition has to distinguish them, preserve legitimate protections, and make the exercise of control answerable to those affected.

The Commercial Age

Exchange · Value moving between participants

Beginnings: exchange is ancient; the age named here emerges as expanding commercial networks increasingly organize wider social life, with a useful European transition window around 1000–1500 CE. Transition: networked computing and the Web in the late twentieth century create foundations for a further shift; language-model agents make that possibility more concrete in the 2020s. [4] [5] [6]

The Commercial Age organizes life around exchange. People who do not share a family, a ruler, or a local history can still offer one another something valuable. Prices, contracts, credit, and commercial networks make it possible to collaborate across distance and difference.

This is a profound expansion of agency. A person can develop a capability, find someone who needs it, and exchange its value for the means to pursue other purposes. Trade can carry possibility beyond the boundaries into which a person was born.

Yet the promise of free exchange remains only partly realized. Commerce grows through the control structures already in place. Nations and states regulate movement and market access. Institutions establish eligibility. Banks and payment providers control financial connections. Companies and platforms govern channels through which buyers and sellers find one another. International trade itself remains shaped by tariff and non-tariff measures. [7]

Two people may be ready to trade and still face a corridor of permissions, fees, incompatible systems, and parties whose consent they must obtain. In this sense, much of commercial life is exchange inside administered enclosures. The market connects the pockets, but the owners of the passages can shape the flow and take a share of what moves.

The deeper limitation is that a transaction sees only part of a relationship. A price can express scarcity and willingness to pay. It carries less of the context of care, trust, long obligation, ecological dependence, or the consequences that fall on people outside the bargain. Commercial success can grow while the relationships sustaining it weaken.

Relatedness remains underneath: the trusted supplier, the loyal collaborator, the customer who becomes a friend, the community that makes a business possible. The opportunity is to give that context a stronger role in how exchange is organized and where its value goes.

The Agentic Age

Coordination · Relatedness capable of scale

Beginnings: an emerging possibility in the 2020s, built on earlier computing and network infrastructure. Transition onward: no established successor or historical end date; the transition now under way concerns how agency will be distributed within the layers we already inhabit. The “Agentic Age” is this paper's proposal for that possibility. [6]

Intelligent agents can help people carry purpose, context, memory, and authorized capacity into networks too complex to navigate unaided. They can discover opportunities, interpret requirements, compare alternatives, prepare agreements, coordinate schedules, and carry out work within a mandate. Research combining language-model reasoning with actions in external environments offers one technical landmark in that development. [6]

The civilizational possibility comes from connecting these capacities through accountable relationships. A person can remain the source of intention while their Allies help extend their reach. A community can govern shared Actors. Many organizations can coordinate through agreements without becoming departments of one organization.

Agency will interweave with kinship, control, and commerce. It can help families care across distance, help workers and communities understand and change institutional rules, and help producers and buyers organize flows around shared purposes. Where participants possess the authority, they can redesign a process. Where law or another party governs the boundary, they can negotiate, seek reform, or choose another lawful arrangement.

The goal is to bring relatedness to the surface of coordination: who is involved, what matters to them, what they are offering, what they have committed to, and who will experience the consequences. Control becomes bounded and accountable. Exchange becomes situated within a richer field of purpose and obligation. People gain the practical means to act together.

The promise of the Agentic Age is relatedness at scale: the freedom to form relationships and the power to make them effective.

This outcome is a choice. The same intelligence can automate institutional control and intensify commercial extraction. A world of more capable agents becomes more agentic for people only when those people can direct the agents, inspect their actions, change the rules, and withdraw authority.

III.

The Architecture of Agency

People want to make something, protect something, change something, or belong to something. Those purposes become powerful when people find others who care, discover what they can contribute, and establish a way to act together.

Kiduna begins there. Massively-multiplayer AI means many people, with distinct purposes and intelligent Allies, participating in a shared world they can help shape. They form relationships, create projects, exchange resources, govern organizations, and coordinate with others whose agents answer to different people. The architecture must keep each participant's context and authority intact as the number of participants grows.

Four inseparable dimensions make that possible: Technology determines what can happen; Governance determines who may decide; Economics determines how resources circulate; Culture gives participation meaning. Relatedness runs through all four.

The architecture is cellular. A person, relationship, project, organization, or ecosystem needs context, memory, boundaries, and ways to respond. These contexts can overlap and connect while retaining distinct purposes and authority.

In Kiduna, Realms hold the contexts in which people and work come together. A relationship can have shared memory without absorbing the private lives of its participants. These capacities make relatedness operational. A commitment can retain its parties, purpose, conditions, and history. A proposed action can be evaluated in the context where it belongs. Agency in practice

A working architecture must do more than introduce people or generate recommendations. It needs ways to sustain shared work, make collective choices, and carry obligations beyond the screen. Economics, governance, and legal standing give coordination material consequence.

The economics of agency

Compute is prepaid capacity for operating intelligence and its supporting services. Within Kiduna's resource model, it is a form of consumable Capacity. Its use pays for the capabilities through which people and collectives work.

The economic design connects that activity to the people who make it possible. Organizers form and sustain relationships. Creators contribute knowledge, methods, media, and experiences. Builders supply tools, agents, and infrastructure. Catalysts bring new work into being. Luminaries contribute bodies of wisdom or creative work, while Stewards maintain the relationships and capabilities on which continuing participation depends.

Agreed allocations can compensate attributable contributions, support shared infrastructure, and return resources to a Realm's treasury. The particular terms belong in inspectable agreements and governance. A title, an invitation, or the purchase of Compute does not itself create an entitlement to earnings or authority.

This connects the Commercial and Kinship layers through Agentic coordination. Exchange sustains the people and relationships that produce value. Shared resources can fund care, creative work, maintenance, learning, and the next collective undertaking. The people involved can see how their activity supports the whole and decide how those flows should change.

Capital can help a project become possible. It must not silently acquire control over the people, their information, or their decisions.

Governance Forums

Coordination needs legitimate ways to disagree. People must be able to question a proposal, change its terms, understand its consequences, and decide whether to proceed.

Kiduna's proposed Governance Forums give members equal governance allocations that cannot be enlarged by purchasing Compute. Envoys are delegated Actors that can research, negotiate, propose, and take positions within explicit mandates. Members establish what their Envoys may do, with which resources, under what limits, and for how long.

A Forum can use structured deliberation and decision markets to expose differences, revise proposals, and seek an agreement. Members can inspect the arguments and the record of how positions changed. They retain the ability to steer or overrule their Envoys before ratification under the Forum's rules.

A ratified decision can authorize work, allocate a budget, assign responsibility, or establish standing policy. Routine actions can then proceed within that policy. Greater consequences can require additional approval, multiple signatures, or a further decision.

Here the control layer is reorganized around accountable delegation. Authority has a source, a scope, a record, and a way to be challenged. Intelligence reduces the burden of participation while people retain authorship of the collective's direction.

Collective agency with legal standing

A group acting through software still needs a recognizable way to hold property, enter agreements, and answer for its conduct. Legal standing connects the collective's internal decisions to obligations in the wider world.

West Virginia's DUNA law provides one such form. It defines a decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association around at least one hundred members joined by mutual consent for a common nonprofit purpose, with an election to operate under the Act. The law recognizes separate legal existence and the capacity to acquire and hold property. [8] [9]

That is a concrete example of the ages interweaving. A legal structure supplies continuity and accountability. Commercial agreements bring resources into the work. Member governance determines how those capacities serve the people and purpose gathered together.

Kinship Duna is the genesis organization described in the published nightpaper. The wider design is an ecology in which independently governed organizations can cooperate through the Kinship Agency Protocol, establish agreements, and use shared capabilities without transferring all authority to one operator.

Companies, universities, foundations, governments, and other institutions can supply services, sponsor work, contract with collectives, or participate through a Realm's legitimate arrangements. Their actual rights depend on those arrangements. The design commitment is that money, infrastructure, and technical capability cannot create ungranted authority over participants.

Institutions can become instruments through which people carry their purposes into the world. The test is whether the relationships affected by their actions can meaningfully govern those actions.

IV.

Machineries of Care

Every system of coordination shapes what people notice, what they can do, and what they learn to expect. When the system serves objectives that participants cannot inspect or change, intelligence can make control more intimate and less visible.

Care asks something more demanding: what conditions help these people perceive clearly, choose freely, grow capable, sustain their relationships, and pursue purposes they recognize as their own?

Bringing relatedness to the surface means making its needs and obligations intelligible to those entitled to act on them. It does not require making private relationships visible to an institution, an advertiser, or the public.

Personal, Enterprise, and Kinship Agents

A personal agent serves an individual. An enterprise agent serves an institution. A kinship agent participates in a relational architecture: its work has an accountable human or collectively governed mandate, a context in which it belongs, and boundaries around the information and authority it may use.

In Kiduna, Allies form governed companion relationships with people or Realms. People can create multiple Allies for different purposes, with defined responsibilities and permissions for each. Actors perform defined functions, including research, operations, negotiation, and stewardship of coherence. Envoys act within delegated positions. No agent acquires authority simply because it can reason, plan, or use a tool.

That distinction matters when a request crosses a boundary. An agent may be able to read a message without being authorized to follow its instructions. It may understand a private fact without having permission to disclose it. It may prepare an agreement without having authority to bind its parties.

Effective coordination requires those distinctions to survive every handoff. The system must preserve who asked, what was authorized, which context applies, and what evidence supports the proposed action. Intelligence helps interpret; enforceable checks constrain execution.

How the Architecture of Agency Is Expressed

The Field is the operating environment where people, relationships, work, resources, and consequences remain connected. People are Sources of intention and authority. Avatars express their presence within a Realm. Allies and Actors extend authorized capacity.

Ki is the continuous Ecosystem intelligence present with Sources throughout Field. It helps carry authorized context between conversations, Realms, and activities, and can hand work to authorized Allies or Actors. It does not independently create consent, permissions, or authority.

A signal can therefore arrive with provenance and land in the context where it matters. The affected people can understand it, consider a response, authorize work, and receive a record of what happened. Relatedness supplies meaning throughout the process.

Privacy as architecture

Public information may circulate openly. Private information moves through permission. Secret information cannot be discovered unless deliberately revealed. Personal information remains with the individual for whom it is held. These boundaries let relationships deepen without making every participant transparent to the whole network.

A project can know that a member is available without knowing why another time is unavailable. A supplier can receive an authorized order without obtaining the membership's private discussions. An ecosystem can support coordination among organizations without collecting an intimate behavioral profile of everyone involved.

Shared learning must have a governed purpose and appropriate consent. Participants determine what may be combined, who may inspect it, how long it may be retained, and what uses remain prohibited. Scaling relatedness requires selective, accountable connection. Trust grows when people can understand and govern the boundary.

Sentinels

Sentinels are coherence Actors with explicit mandates. They can help notice unsupported claims, unauthorized actions, manipulation, neglected commitments, or departures from a Realm's published principles. Their observations and interventions remain bounded by scope, evidence, privacy, and policy.

A Sentinel may ask for clarification, surface a concern, or hold an action when its mandate permits. It cannot infer unlimited powers from the importance of care, invent a hidden assessment of a person, or silently turn its own interpretation into the rules everyone must follow.

People need to know why an intervention appeared, what informed it, and how to question it. They need ways to revise permissions, revoke delegations, appeal decisions, repair relationships, and leave. Departure must preserve legitimate obligations and records while making identity, work, and permitted history portable.

Care without agency becomes paternalism.

Agency without care becomes abandonment.

A family, a research group, a cooperative, and a watershed council will develop different practices. The architecture should support that variety while keeping consequences across boundaries accountable. Scaling relatedness means enabling many living contexts to cooperate without requiring them to become culturally identical.

V.

Agency in Action

Consider a community that wants to restore its watershed.

The work begins in relatedness. People care about a place, its water, its creatures, their neighbors, and the lives that will follow theirs. Local knowledge and shared concern give the undertaking meaning.

It encounters control. Land ownership, access rights, public responsibilities, permits, and organizational policies shape what may happen. These boundaries need to be understood, and some may need to be changed through legitimate processes.

It requires exchange. Equipment, professional knowledge, transport, materials, funding, and labor must reach the work on terms the participants can sustain. Some contributions may be purchased, others shared or given. Every flow has conditions and consequences.

Agentic coordination can connect the layers. Allies help people develop the purpose and find collaborators. Researchers assemble relevant evidence. Envoys explore agreements within their mandates. Members govern priorities and budgets. Authorized Actors coordinate the approved work. Sentinels help surface concerns. Records preserve decisions and results so participants can learn.

The watershed's interests must also enter the decision. Accountable people can represent ecological limits and long obligations, supported by evidence and explicit stewardship commitments. People who are absent, downstream, or not yet born cannot be treated as irrelevant because they did not participate in the transaction.

No agent can make a permit disappear or declare someone else's land available. It can make the conditions intelligible, prepare the work needed to meet them, help the parties negotiate, and reveal where a structure is frustrating a purpose it should serve. Over time, the people with authority can reorganize that structure.

Relatedness becomes more powerful because governance and exchange become easier to coordinate around it. The law carries commitments. Commercial flows sustain contribution. Intelligence extends the capacity of the people doing the work. Experience returns to the relationships from which the purpose arose.

This is the larger invitation of Kiduna. The four ages remain present, but their relationship can change. Control can become more answerable. Exchange can become more direct, contextual, and sustaining. Relatedness can move beyond isolated pockets into a connected field of purposeful action.

The promise will be realized through practice: whether people can act together across greater complexity, whether they retain meaningful choice, whether they can repair what fails, and whether their growing collective capacity strengthens the lives within it.

Relatedness gives us reasons to act together.

Agency gives us the capacity.

Coordination lets that capacity scale.

A civilization capable of coordinating freely and powerfully begins by keeping the people and relationships that give it life at the center of what it does.

II. Economics — The Means to Act

Keeping people and relationships at the center of coordination requires more than recognizing their importance. People need time, tools, knowledge, and the means to sustain the work they undertake together. A watershed project needs research, equipment, permits, and people who can keep showing up. A film needs a script, a crew, an audience, and a way to pay for the months between an idea and something others can experience. Intelligence can help organize these relationships. An economy gives them continuity.

Kiduna's economic purpose is to turn useful participation into greater capacity to act. The app should help someone move from “I want to do this” to an intelligible account of what it requires, who can contribute, what it will cost, how it will be funded, and who will receive the value created. Money becomes part of the work's visible structure. It does not need to become the reason for every relationship.

That distinction matters from the first screen. Someone arriving to learn, make something, organize a project, or participate in an event should encounter that purpose. They should be able to see what is available, try what can be offered without charge, and understand a paid action before committing to it. An unfamiliar financial instrument should not be the entrance examination for using helpful software.

Paying for useful work

Kiduna uses Compute to measure the economic cost of operating intelligence and its supporting services. A request may require model inference, information retrieval, storage, processing, an external service, or several of these together. Compute brings those costs into an understandable service price. The person using the app sees what the work will cost, not a bill assembled from a dozen infrastructure vendors after the work is finished.

The supplied economic design distinguishes this unit of measurement from $KIDUNA, the transferable token used to settle payments across the network. Compute answers “What does this work cost?” The token answers “What asset is used to pay for it?” The distinction clarifies the opening paper's broad description of prepaid capacity: holding KIDUNA makes a settlement asset available, but does not guarantee a fixed dollar amount of future service. A token balance and a fixed-value service credit are different promises. Economic design, §§3, 9, 36

The app should show both the token amount and its current estimated purchasing power, with the price and time of that estimate. Before a paid action begins, it should present a bounded quote. A person authorizing a dollar of work should know how many tokens will be charged, when the quote expires, whether partial work can incur costs, and what happens if the task needs more than expected.

If an organization separately sells a fixed-value service credit, the app must identify who owes the service, where it can be used, and its refund and expiry terms. That obligation belongs on the issuer's books. It cannot disappear because the same screen also contains a token wallet. Simplicity in the interface should come from a clear model underneath it.

The default price in the supplied design is ten times the underlying infrastructure cost, called the Agency Multiplier. An operation costing $0.10 therefore has a $1.00 service price before discounts. The difference supports organizing, contribution, continuing development, and financial reserves. Each application can establish different prices under its organization's published terms, provided mandatory costs and compensation remain funded.

For an infrastructure cost C, multiplier m, and consumption discount d, the service price is R = C × m × (1 − d). At a token price P dollars per KIDUNA, the payment is R ÷ P tokens, rounded to the token's six-decimal precision. The implementation must use exact integer or fixed-point arithmetic and make any rounding rule inspectable.

At the proposed genesis reference price of $0.00001 per token, $1.00 of work costs 100,000 KIDUNA. If the token price halves, the same dollar-priced work costs 200,000. The service tariff can remain stable while a previously acquired token balance buys less. An honest balance display must make this visible without turning the app into a trading terminal.

An economy organized around use

A person can buy KIDUNA from an authorized primary distribution, acquire it on a public market, receive it from someone else, or earn it for a contribution. None of those events determines which application will eventually provide a service. Buying the asset and using it are separate transactions.

When someone pays for work in an application, the payment belongs to the organization responsible for that work. The network operator receives the infrastructure and service fees established by agreement. It does not acquire the remainder merely because it provides the software or introduced the participants.

This relationship should be visible at checkout and in the receipt. The app identifies the service, the organization providing it, the approved price, the person or organization paying, and any agreed allocation across contributors or applications. It establishes that attribution before execution. Revenue should not be redirected afterward by whichever party controls the reporting system.

Consider the watershed project from the opening section. Its members commission a research summary using a method developed by one contributor and software maintained by another. The project can pay from an approved budget. The receipt records what was used, who supplied it, the infrastructure consumed, and the resulting allocations. A later payment for field equipment remains an equipment purchase; it does not automatically become Compute revenue. Different transactions retain their actual meaning.

In the proposed default, the service payment first establishes any eligible organizing compensation, then covers infrastructure, leaving Net Agency Revenue. That remainder is allocated 40% to contribution compensation, 50% to the organization's Agency Pool, and 10% to its Resilience Reserve. These are allocations of the remainder, not percentages added on top of the customer's price.

The people who make the work possible

Organizing is valuable when it helps people participate in something useful. An invitation can begin that relationship, but the invitation itself does not justify a perpetual claim on another person's economic life.

The supplied design ties organizing compensation to actual qualifying paid use within the relevant organization. It provides four generations of invitation attribution: 20%, 5%, 3%, and 2% on initial paid participation, then 12%, 3%, 1.8%, and 1.2% on continuing participation for 18 months. Those totals are 30% and 18%. The automatic continuing allocation ends after the stated period. Token acquisition and token holding earn no organizing payment. Economic design, §19

The arrangement needs precise boundaries before it becomes a feature in the app. This draft proposes that initial participation mean the first settled, non-refunded paid service transaction in that organization, with the 18-month period beginning at its timestamp. Missing or ineligible invitation positions would leave their share with the organization rather than increase the share of another generation. These clarifications require adoption in the final terms; they are not established by the PDF alone.

Self-referrals, circular invitations, simulated customers, test activity, and work created principally to manufacture compensation are excluded. A refund reverses the associated accrual. A correction to invitation history should not silently redirect money already earned. The person using the service should be able to inspect the applicable arrangement without disclosing their private work to everyone in the invitation chain.

The four-generation structure also requires a specific legal review. Paying on real service use does not, by itself, establish that a compensation program is lawful. The FTC's guidance examines the incentives and actual practices of multi-level arrangements, including pressure to recruit or purchase. The operating terms, earnings claims, training, and user experience must be assessed together. No purchase quota, recruitment requirement, or promised investment recovery should be built into participation. FTC guidance

Contribution compensation follows a different logic. People create knowledge, experiences, tools, methods, and infrastructure that others find useful. The initial Compensation Pool allocates 20% to creation, 20% to technical work, 10% to substantial intellectual or cultural contributions, 25% to founding work, and 25% to valuable contributions that do not fit a predetermined category.

For Kinship Duna, the supplied design caps the Catalyst team's allocation at $50,000 per month; any excess goes to Adaptive Contribution. This is a ceiling on an earned allocation, not a guaranteed monthly payment. Separately attributable work can receive separate compensation, but the same contribution cannot be counted twice under different titles. Beneficiaries, conflicts, duration, and any protected founding agreement must be visible before participation.

Every two weeks, software can prepare a proposed distribution using verified use, attributable work, efficiency, satisfaction, and outcomes. Members review the proposal and approve the resulting payments through the organization's governance. The app should let a contributor follow a payment back to its evidence, see why a contribution was excluded, and challenge an error before the distribution becomes final.

Consumption alone is an inadequate measure. A useful tool may reduce model calls, finish work faster, or prevent unnecessary work altogether. Paying only for volume would punish efficiency and reward waste. The distribution method must distinguish genuine demand, useful completion, maintenance, and cost reduction from repeated activity that merely produces more billable events. Careful human review remains necessary where value cannot be measured mechanically.

Why a common token

A shared settlement asset can connect independently governed organizations without requiring each to negotiate a separate payment relationship with every other participant. It can move between a person's wallet, a project budget, a contributor payment, and an organization's treasury. A finite network reserve can help fund new operating capacity under published allocation rules.

Those functions explain the proposal. They do not establish that every useful interaction needs a token. Dollar payments and stablecoins can pay many of the same bills. The supplied model already permits shared infrastructure obligations to be settled in KIDUNA and/or USDC. The test is whether common settlement, contribution flows, and independent operation create enough value to justify the extra liquidity, custody, pricing, and legal complexity.

The app should absorb avoidable complexity without concealing material facts. People need understandable payment choices, a way to fund a bounded amount of work, and a receipt they can reconcile. They do not need to understand a liquidity pool before asking for a research summary. When a conversion is necessary, its rate, fees, and limits should be part of the quote.

More useful activity does not mechanically produce a higher token price. Recipients may spend or sell what they receive. Faster circulation can support more work with a smaller average balance. New distributions can increase transferable supply. The value of the app must therefore be demonstrated through use and outcomes, separately from the market price of its settlement asset.

A reserve with accountable stewards

KAD's proposed role is narrow: approve allocations to recognized operating ecosystems under published capitalization plans. It does not thereby govern the projects or organizations using the software. Each allocation should be justified by expected service demand, infrastructure needs, liquidity, existing inventory, and a budget. Further tranches require an account of what happened to the previous one.

The supplied governance begins with a founding operator and adds an operator position for each independent ecosystem. Once three or more exist, new allocations require at least two-thirds approval, rounded upward. The period with two operators, conflicts of interest, recognition criteria, operator removal, and release limits still require explicit rules.

This draft proposes a public allocation calendar, a seven-day delay between approval and execution of a tranche, and monthly reconciliation of reserve wallets. A period-wide release cap must be adopted before the TGE. Recognition must examine actual independence and beneficial control; one controller cannot acquire several votes merely by registering several operating names.

The initial concentration should be described candidly. Several signing devices are useful security, but do not distribute authority when one person controls them all. Transition means a real change in who can decide and execute, evidenced by legal documents, member decisions, and wallet controls.

Liquidity without a promise of redemption

Public liquidity provides a conversion opportunity. It does not promise that everyone can exit at the displayed price. The initial KIDUNA/USDC position belongs to Kinship Duna and, under the supplied model, cannot be used for normal operations. There is no unlimited obligation to redeem tokens from the operating treasury.

The proposed initial position contains 250,000 USDC. In an illustrative constant-product pool with 25 billion KIDUNA and no fees or other trading, a sale of 1 billion tokens returns about $9,615 rather than its $10,000 starting reference value. A sale of 5 billion returns about $41,667 rather than $50,000. Selling 25 billion returns $125,000 and leaves the marginal token price at one-quarter of its starting value.

The illustration is not a selection of a particular exchange or liquidity design. A concentrated-liquidity position can behave differently, including becoming inactive outside its price range. The launch terms must specify venue, curve, fees, ranges, position ownership, withdrawal authority, and rebalancing rules. Nominal pool value is not the amount holders can collectively withdraw in dollars.

After market trading begins, direct primary sales must use an approved market-derived price rather than undercut public trading with discounted transferable tokens. Twenty percent of future direct primary-sale proceeds remains designated for liquidity, with matching token inventory reserved. The app must distinguish primary purchases, market purchases, and service payments, since their economic destinations differ.

Service quotes need both a trustworthy reference price and evidence that required external costs can be funded. A time-weighted price can reduce sensitivity to an isolated trade, but a thin pool can be manipulated over a sustained interval. Freshness, depth, deviation, maximum transaction size, and executable slippage limits must be tested together. If a reliable price or funded conversion is unavailable, automatic paid work pauses or offers a separately authorized payment route.

USDC itself introduces dependence on its issuer, banking arrangements, and blocking powers. Its dollar reference does not make it equivalent to unrestricted cash under every circumstance. Keeping the organization able to continue

The Agency Pool finances the organization's purpose: development, useful grants, support, research, events, infrastructure, and other authorized work. The Resilience Reserve protects continuity. The supplied target is at least six months of trailing infrastructure costs and committed operating obligations. Once that target is reached, new 10% allocations default to the Agency Pool unless governance chooses another rule; existing resilience assets remain restricted.

The reserve should be measured in assets that can actually pay those obligations. This draft proposes excluding unconverted KIDUNA inventory from minimum hard-cost cover and using the greater of trailing average obligations and known forward commitments. Restricted liquidity cannot also be counted as freely available operations funding. Every asset can be earmarked honestly only once.

At $100,000 in monthly service receipts, a 10× effective multiplier and an 18% organizing rate leave $72,000 in Net Agency Revenue: $28,800 for compensation, $36,000 for agency, and $7,200 for resilience. Infrastructure costs are $10,000. If other monthly operating commitments are $40,000, the Agency allocation alone is $4,000 short. Money in restricted pools cannot silently close that gap.

Six months of those $50,000 obligations requires $300,000. Building it from zero at $7,200 per month would take about 41.7 months, assuming unchanged activity and no withdrawals. Genesis capital therefore has to fund early continuity; future reserve contributions are not a substitute for an opening cash plan.

The $1 million operating allocation could support 20 months at $50,000 monthly net cash burn or ten months at $100,000, before separately incurred one-time costs. If $200,000 is held unavailable for routine use, the spendable $800,000 supports 16 or eight months respectively. These are planning illustrations, not a claim about actual staffing or approved expenditure.

Before launch, the budget must identify engineering, security, legal work, infrastructure, support, pilot activity, compensation commitments, and contingency. It should be tested against a sharp decline in paid use, a token price fall, higher inference costs, loss of a service provider, and temporary unavailability of a settlement asset. The point of a reserve is to preserve room for deliberate choice when the forecast is wrong.

A market that begins with a useful app

Kiduna's initial market consists of people doing work that repeatedly crosses conversation, knowledge, decisions, contributions, and payment. Research groups, independent production teams, member-governed organizations, learning programs, events, and collaborative projects all experience the cost of repeatedly assembling context across disconnected tools.

The value proposition is practical. A person brings work into the app, understands it with assistance, connects it to the people and materials that matter, makes a decision, and carries that decision into action with a record of the result. The app can host different kinds of activities while preserving this continuity. Its breadth becomes useful when people can accomplish their particular task without first learning the whole system.

Existing collaboration suites, payment tools, agent frameworks, voting systems, and creator platforms already solve parts of this problem. Kiduna must earn adoption by making the whole sequence easier and more accountable. A narrow, well-understood workflow may be better served by a simpler tool. Interoperability and export make it possible to complement those tools instead of requiring an immediate replacement of everything a group uses.

The Big Kiduna event can be an initial test of this experience: people find relevant sessions and one another, work with shared material, make introductions with consent, and carry useful relationships into projects afterward. Registration is only a beginning. The stronger evidence is what participants accomplish and whether they continue using the app when the event ends.

For illustration, ten organizations with 100 monthly paying participants each, spending $20 after discounts, generate $20,000 of monthly service receipts. One hundred generate $200,000. These are arithmetic scenarios, not market forecasts. Reports must remove duplicated spend, separate sponsored and unsubsidized use, and distinguish product revenue from token-sale proceeds.

Meaningful adoption evidence includes time to first useful result, repeated paid use, retention after subsidies end, task completion, effective prices, support burden, acquisition cost, and contribution margin. The app should also measure whether participants understand their costs and permissions. A high conversion rate obtained through confusion would be a failure of the design.

An open-source product cannot rely on making departure difficult. Its durable value must come from useful capabilities, reliable operation, trusted relationships, and improvements that participants want to keep using. Economic success should expand the means to act. That brings the next question into focus: who decides how those means are used?

III. Governance — The Right to Decide

Resources create possibilities. They also create power. A treasury can fund a project, determine whose work continues, and shape what an organization becomes. Software that helps people accumulate that capacity must give them an equally practical way to govern it.

Kiduna's governance begins with the distinction between having a stake in a decision and owning a settlement asset. In Kinship Duna, purchasing more KIDUNA does not purchase a larger vote. Capital can support useful work without becoming the source of authority over the people doing it.

The app should make this separation ordinary. A person opens a proposal because it affects their organization or commitments, not because they hold enough tokens to unlock the voting screen. Participation needs to be possible for someone who has time to read carefully and for someone who needs a concise explanation, accessible audio, translation, or help examining a difficult argument.

Membership before ballots

Equal member governance requires a credible account of membership. A wallet proves control of a key; it does not prove that the holder is a unique human, eligible to vote, or free from coercion. Several wallets or profiles cannot create several votes for one person.

The organization must publish how people join, what they agree to, how eligibility is verified, and how admission or exclusion can be challenged. This draft proposes a privacy-conscious process combining authenticated identity, accountable attestations, duplicate checks, and human review of contested cases. It does not assume biometric collection. The chosen implementation must disclose its data requirements, error rates, retention, recovery, and appeal procedures before it governs real resources.

Natural persons hold the member vote in the proposed genesis organization. Companies, universities, funders, and service providers can contract, sponsor work, and contribute expertise. Their actual authority comes from an explicit legal and organizational arrangement. Funding does not silently create membership, access to private discussions, or control over the people receiving support.

The electorate for a proposal is established at a published point in time. Changes to membership cannot be used to alter an inconvenient vote after it has begun. A contested admission or suspension needs a procedure that preserves fairness without allowing the entire organization to be held indefinitely in uncertainty.

Assistance without surrender

Most people cannot study every proposal in full. A working governance system has to take that fact seriously without pretending that absence means agreement.

An authorized agent can help a member understand a proposal, compare its effects with prior commitments, research alternatives, ask questions, and negotiate revisions. The member sets its mandate: what matters to them, which decisions it may address, what resources it may use, what limits it must respect, and when the delegation expires. The app should make those instructions visible and easy to change.

Delegation can have different degrees. Someone may authorize research only, permit negotiation without a vote, or allow an agent to submit a position within stated limits. A direct human decision supersedes the delegated position before the voting deadline. Revoking a mandate stops future use; it does not rewrite the historical record of what was previously authorized.

The original nightpaper proposes equal governance allocations and decision markets that help expose disagreement and develop proposals. These instruments must remain separate from transferable KIDUNA. This draft proposes non-purchasable, non-transferable deliberation credits reset for each proposal, with no redemption value and no ability to increase a member's final voting weight.

The decision market's price is a signal within deliberation, not evidence that a proposal is true, lawful, or accepted. Its trading rules, budget constraints, manipulation resistance, and relationship to final ratification require further specification and testing. A direct equal-member ballot should remain available as the initial binding decision method. Advanced deliberation should improve the process without becoming a prerequisite for exercising a basic right.

An agent's recommended position can become a member's default ballot only under an active mandate and a visible review window. Without such a mandate or a direct vote, the member is absent. The system must not manufacture participation from silence.

A decision people can follow

The governance experience begins with a clear proposal: what will change, why it matters, who is affected, what it costs, what alternatives were considered, and what authority is being requested. A financial proposal includes the actual destination, amount or spending limit, timing, and conditions. A policy proposal includes the text that would take effect.

The app should offer a short account that leads into the complete evidence, with minority arguments and unresolved questions accessible alongside the recommendation. Summaries must preserve material disagreement. A member should be able to compare versions and see which objections led to a change.

Deliberation ends by freezing a specific version for ratification. The text, electorate, counting rules, executable instructions, and schedule are published together. A material amendment restarts the necessary notice and vote. An attractive summary cannot authorize a different transaction hidden beneath it.

After voting, the app reports participation, support, abstention, and whether the rule was satisfied. A challenge period allows questions about eligibility, conflicts, coercion, or execution fidelity. Only then does the approved instruction become eligible for execution, subject to current legal and technical constraints. The result returns to the same proposal so members can see whether it happened and what followed.

This creates continuity between an intention, a collective choice, and an observable consequence. “Passed” and “completed” are different states. A payment can be approved but blocked by insufficient funds. A policy can be adopted but await an implementation. The interface should make those differences legible.

The quality of this arrangement depends on both control and usability. Too little review exposes members to unauthorized consequences. Too many prompts train them to approve without reading. Permissions should therefore match the actual risk and recurrence of the work, with understandable defaults, visible limits, and an accessible way to pause.

Power that remains answerable

Equal ballots do not remove every form of unequal influence. Founders may control the roadmap. Skilled speakers may dominate discussion. A funder may make refusal costly. Many agents may rely on the same model or inherit the same framing. Members need ways to see and contest these concentrations.

Administrators should have named responsibilities, terms, compensation, conflicts, and removal procedures. Related-party compensation and grants require independent review. A protected founding arrangement must disclose what is protected, for how long, and how it can change. It cannot become an unannounced claim on every future decision.

This draft proposes that the initial administrative mandate expire after six months unless members renew specified powers. The transition requires a functioning electorate, a direct voting path, reconciled accounts, and actual transfer of applicable signing and administrative authority. If those conditions are not met, the organization must disclose the delay and approve a bounded extension. KAD's reserve governance remains a separate legal and operational structure.

Member governance is also bounded. A majority cannot create consent to disclose another person's private information, erase legitimate obligations, or turn a disputed accusation into a fact. Personal assets remain distinguishable from the organizational treasury. Sanctions require notice, reasons, an opportunity to respond, and an appeal to people with legitimate authority and appropriate independence.

Someone affected by a decision may deserve a hearing without being a voting member. Downstream residents, contractors, former participants, and people whose information is involved can have legitimate interests. The app should support receiving their evidence and recording the organization's response. Standing to be heard is not identical to voting membership.

A legal body behind the software

Member decisions need a recognizable legal counterparty when they produce contracts, payments, property rights, employment, or liability. West Virginia's DUNA form is the proposed foundation for Kinship Duna. Its definition requires at least 100 members joined by mutual consent for a common nonprofit purpose and election into the Act. The law provides a distinct liability boundary and requires a service-of-process agent filing. Actual compliance depends on the organization's facts and documents. West Virginia definition, liability, agent filing

The statute permits profit-making activity in furtherance of the common nonprofit purpose and specified compensation and other payments. It restricts ordinary distributions. Contribution payments therefore need a documented purpose, actual services where relevant, reasonable terms, and conflict review. A percentage formula or a role title does not settle the legal question. Nonprofit status also does not automatically establish federal tax exemption. West Virginia compensation provisions

The app should distinguish the person authorizing an action, the organization legally responsible for it, the service provider carrying it out, and the party receiving payment. A person reading an agreement needs to know whom they are contracting with. A participant seeking a remedy needs a reachable process beyond a wallet address.

Governing the launch itself

The TGE requires a complete account of who issues, sells, holds reserves, provides services, operates liquidity, and controls the relevant software and keys. KAD, Kinship Duna, and other operating organizations cannot be treated as interchangeable names. Their agreements, beneficial control, obligations, and decision rights must be verified and disclosed.

The absence of token-weighted voting, dividends, or equity rights does not by itself determine the legal treatment of an offering. The SEC's current interpretive materials distinguish crypto assets from transactions and arrangements involving them. The actual distribution, representations, managerial commitments, and purchaser expectations require qualified analysis under applicable law. SEC interpretation, transaction guidance

Before commitments are accepted, the sale terms must establish the lawful offering route, eligible jurisdictions and participants, opening and closing conditions, minimum viable funding, hard cap, allocation method, delivery, refunds, custody, and required disclosures. The $1.25 million target in the economic design is not a substitute for these terms. If an escrowed round does not meet its conditions, funds must follow the published return process rather than become discretionary operating cash.

Counsel must also address payments and custody, sanctions, taxes, privacy, consumer protection, compensation practices, and any restrictions on transfers or market activity. This document supplies a design to evaluate; it does not declare a legal exemption or a launch authorization.

A launch decision should bind the exact document version, code, addresses, authorities, economic rules, security findings, and budget that were reviewed. Members should be able to open that record from the app and compare subsequent changes with it. Governance is most useful when it continues after the vote, keeping authority connected to what the system actually does.

That connection is a technical obligation. A promise that people remain in control matters only if the software carries their choices faithfully through every handoff.

IV. Technology — Intelligence Made Accountable

The app is where the architecture either becomes useful or remains a theory. Someone should be able to bring in a document, ask a question, find the relevant people, prepare a piece of work, approve a bounded action, and understand the result. The underlying system may be sophisticated. The person's experience should remain coherent.

Kiduna's technical task is to preserve identity, context, permission, and consequence while many people and software processes work together. A conversation is connected to the work it concerns. A proposal is connected to its evidence. A payment is connected to an authorized decision. A result returns to the people entitled to see and use it.

One app, many kinds of work

The app brings conversation, content, people, applications, and decisions into a connected working environment. A person can encounter a document as something to read, a subject to discuss, an input to a task, and a record associated with a project. These uses should refer to the same underlying item instead of producing unrelated copies with different histories.

Context matters more than the number of features visible at once. Opening a project should show its purpose, participants, relevant material, commitments, and current work. Opening a document should reveal its contents, provenance, discussion, and permitted next actions. A concise presentation can expand when the person needs detail without hiding ownership, cost, or authority.

Conversation should help someone operate this environment. “Summarize the changes,” “find the decision that authorized this,” or “prepare a proposal for next month's budget” should resolve to specific material and actions. The system should show what it understood and which information it used. Ambiguity that changes the affected people, money, or disclosure requires clarification.

The same person may participate in several organizations with different responsibilities. The app must make the active context visible and avoid carrying permissions or confidential information across it. Switching from personal planning to an organizational budget should not silently reuse the authority of the previous task.

Small-screen participation and sustained desktop work have different needs. Mobile should support quick understanding, communication, bounded approvals, and capture of useful information. Desktop can expose more evidence, comparison, authoring, and administration. They share underlying identity and records. Moving between them must not lose a draft or change what an approval means.

A concrete system boundary

The proposed implementation separates persistent information, intelligence, policy enforcement, financial accounting, and settlement. This separation reduces the number of things a model can accidentally or maliciously control.

Persistent information includes documents, messages, people, organizations, projects, commitments, and their typed relationships. Each item has a stable identity, version history, provenance, access policy, and retention rule. Search and generated views are projections of that information, not independent authorities that can overwrite it.

Intelligence retrieves permitted material, reasons about the task, and proposes work. It can use approved tools within a mandate. It does not hold an unrestricted treasury key or decide for itself that a private document is safe to publish.

The policy layer checks exact actions against current authority, permissions, consent, budgets, and constraints. The accounting layer records quotes, reserved funds, metered usage, earned revenue, obligations, refunds, and distributions. Settlement transfers assets through the selected payment and wallet infrastructure. A durable execution record connects all five.

The PDF's Squads treasury design is consistent with an initial Solana deployment, but the chain, program versions, and mainnet addresses remain to be established in a release manifest. The token supply's 10 trillion units at six decimals equal 10 quintillion atomic units. That fits an unsigned 64-bit supply field, while intermediate arithmetic may require wider precision. These amounts must never be handled as ordinary floating-point JavaScript numbers. Solana token documentation

The launch must verify the entire mint, its supply, and removal of mint authority. Freeze authority, metadata authority, delegates, transfer hooks or fees, and program upgrade powers require a separate inventory. Removing one power does not remove the others. A replacement token cannot silently inherit the original's identity or fixed-supply claim.

From incoming information to an authorized action

A message, document, event, or external response enters the app as attributable information. It may be useful, mistaken, malicious, or irrelevant. Receipt means that the system has accepted it for evaluation. It does not mean the statement is true or the request is authorized.

The system preserves the original, records its origin and context, and creates a permitted view for the relevant destination. A summary or translation retains a relationship to the original rather than replacing it. An assertion can be connected to a project, decision, or document while retaining its evidentiary status. The app should let a person inspect that relationship and correct it.

This is the practical meaning of the current Kiduna Transceiver specification: delivery, interpretation, attention, acceptance, and execution are separate steps. A useful notification can bring a matter forward; it cannot turn attention into consent. An imported document may contain instructions for its reader; those instructions do not automatically become authority for the software processing it.

When a consequential action is proposed, its record identifies who is acting, on whose behalf, the active organization or personal context, target, exact parameters, intended effects, authority references, quote, maximum spend, expiry, and required evidence. An approval binds those fields. Changing the recipient or enlarging the amount creates a different action.

Immediately before execution, deterministic checks verify current permission, revocation, policy, funds, and limits. A standing authorization can allow recurring work without repeated confirmation. It remains a specific grant, not the disappearance of the boundary.

The app should explain an action in the terms a person needs to decide: “Send this document to these recipients,” “Pay this supplier up to this amount,” or “Run this task until this budget is used.” The explanation leads to complete details. The approval is neither a vague “continue” button nor an invitation to read raw program instructions.

Work that fails honestly

External systems do not always respond cleanly. A payment can succeed while a network connection times out. A research task can complete half its work before a provider fails. A service can incur costs while producing an unusable result. Reliable software records these states instead of forcing them into “done” or “failed.”

Each execution has a durable idempotency identifier so a retry does not duplicate an effect. Before repeating a payment or external instruction, the system checks the previous state and available receipt. An ambiguous outcome remains “confirmation pending” or “outcome unknown” until reconciliation establishes what happened.

Payment and an external service call cannot generally be made one atomic transaction. The proposed pattern is to reserve a bounded amount, perform the authorized work, settle verified usage, and release the remainder. Partial completion follows the published charging and refund policy. Reversing an accounting entry does not make an email unsent or a supplier's work undone.

People need useful recovery paths. They should see what completed, what remains, which funds are reserved, and whether another attempt can create additional cost or effects. Support staff need sufficient records to investigate without gaining unrestricted access to unrelated personal information.

Knowledge with boundaries

Permission must be enforced before information reaches search results, a model prompt, a cache, a generated summary, or a third-party service. Filtering the final answer is too late if the model or provider has already received material it was not entitled to process.

The data model therefore binds visibility and permitted use to the item and its derivatives. Retrieval operates within the active person's and organization's authorized context. Shared work can use shared information while personal notes remain personal. A supplier can receive delivery instructions without receiving the internal discussion that produced the order.

The opening paper's distinctions among public, private, secret, and personal information imply protection of metadata as well as content. Search counts, visible connections, timing, and suggestions must not reveal the existence of a protected relationship. A hidden document is not protected if the interface announces who is discussing it.

Encryption at rest and in transit is necessary, but it does not by itself make processing private from the operator or model provider. The service must disclose where plaintext exists, who can access it, retention, subprocessors, and whether private inputs are used for training. Stronger guarantees require an architecture that actually prevents the prohibited access.

Permission revocation stops future authorized use according to applicable obligations. It cannot make an already disclosed fact unknown to its recipient. Deletion and export must explain what happens to backups, financial records, shared work, legal holds, and legitimate records held by others. Personal portability does not mean ownership of every conversation in which someone participated.

Public blockchain records should contain only what is justified for settlement and verification. Hashes and commitments can connect private evidence to public events, but a hash proves consistency with bytes, not truth or consent. Sensitive facts with few possible values can be guessed from unsalted public hashes. The privacy model has to account for such inference.

Identity and custody people can use

Account recovery is part of agency. A system that gives someone formal control but makes a lost device equivalent to losing their work and assets has transferred a serious burden to them. Recovery must be usable without creating an undisclosed operator back door.

The supplied design specifies personal FROST wallets. FROST is a threshold signing protocol, not a complete wallet or recovery product. The deployment must specify how keys are generated, how shares are held, what threshold can sign, how devices are replaced, and what happens when a signer is unavailable. Compatibility with the selected chain's signature rules requires testing. RFC 9591

A possible starting design is two-of-three signing across a user device, a separately controlled recovery factor, and a service-assisted signer. The important property is actual independence: the operator must not effectively control two shares. Recovery needs notice, delay appropriate to the risk, a contest procedure, and a documented response to identity theft. Experienced users should have a supported self-custody path where compatible with the service.

Organizational wallets need their own controls. Squads provides treasury transaction capabilities, including timelocks and spending limits, but does not itself establish an organization's membership or legitimate vote. The adapter from member decision to treasury instruction must verify the exact approved payload. Configuration changes, spending allowances, signer replacement, and upgrade authority must be included in the security review. Squads documentation

The interface should expose signing status in ordinary language: approved by members, waiting for the required signatures, delayed until the review period ends, executed, or blocked with a reason. A signer should be able to inspect the human authorization and the actual transfer together. The system should detect a mismatch before funds move.

Threats the architecture must withstand

The combination of private information, untrusted incoming material, and external tools creates an attractive attack surface. Malicious text can try to turn a summarization request into a data disclosure. A forged delegation can try to make one person's agent act for another. An apparently helpful integration can request broader access than its purpose requires.

The controls must therefore operate outside the model's persuasion loop: isolated credentials, constrained network destinations, authenticated tool calls, exact action schemas, revocable capabilities, budget limits, and deterministic authorization. A safety-oriented model can help detect trouble, but it is not the sole security boundary.

Other threats include duplicate identities, coordinated voting, signer collusion, manipulated prices, compromised dependencies, replayed transactions, data leakage across organizations, and unavailable infrastructure. Security review must follow the complete path from identity and quote through execution and reconciliation. An audit of an underlying wallet program does not establish that Kiduna's integration uses it safely.

Before launch, independent review should cover token authorities, reserve controls, wallet recovery, the governance adapter, service metering, price references, settlement, and privacy boundaries. Unresolved critical or high findings block release. Other findings need owners, mitigations, and disclosed residual risk. A vulnerability reporting process and incident runbook identify who can pause affected functions, notify people, preserve evidence, and authorize restoration.

Emergency powers must be narrow enough to test. They may stop new vulnerable transactions or revoke a compromised permission. They do not authorize a new distribution, unlimited data access, or indefinite suspension of member governance. The app records why the pause occurred and what evidence is required to end it.

Open operation and independent organizations

The software is intended to be open source so other operators can inspect, run, and improve it. A release needs a license, reproducible build instructions, dependency versions, security contacts, and an account of components that remain proprietary or externally supplied. “Open source” does not make all deployments equally secure or all data public.

Interoperability requires more than agents exchanging messages. Each service must publish its identity, supported versions, permitted capabilities, settlement routes, and dispute contacts. A cross-organization request carries its mandate, expiry, minimal necessary context, attribution, and expected receipt. Agent communication standards can provide transport and discovery; they do not create membership or authority. A2A specification

Organizations can use KIDUNA without issuing their own token. Those that choose a local settlement asset must publish its rights, supply, issuance powers, distribution, liquidity, and conversion rules. They remain responsible for paying shared infrastructure from liquid funds. If their asset cannot support a trustworthy quote or conversion, the app offers funded KIDUNA payment or pauses the work. It does not spend a different asset without permission or pass an unfunded liability to the network.

An organization changing infrastructure operators needs an authorized migration, settlement of old obligations, destination acceptance, transfer of permitted data, reconfiguration of keys and permissions, and a clear cutover for in-flight work. Its treasury and membership do not become the old operator's property. Existing contracts continue to matter.

A software fork likewise does not transfer private data, intellectual property rights, another organization's legal identity, or its assets. The freedom to leave is supported through usable export, documented interfaces, and honest contracts. Portability should be rehearsed before participants depend on it.

Reliability before scale claims

The initial deployment will have central dependencies: model providers, identity services, application hosting, settlement infrastructure, operators, and legal counterparties. They must be visible. The path toward independent operation should be measured through actual changes in deployment and control, not described as completed because the architecture permits them.

This draft proposes starting service targets of 99.9% monthly availability for authentication, quoting, and permission checks; p95 policy decisions within 500 milliseconds excluding model work at a declared workload; and private-ledger recovery within four hours with at most five minutes of recoverable application-state lag. These are targets to validate, not observed performance. Financial records still must reconcile against finalized settlement with no duplicated or missing charges.

Testing must include revocation between approval and execution, uncertain external outcomes, duplicate requests, lost devices, provider outages, stale prices, insufficient funds, refunds across payout periods, and cross-organization access attempts. Load results should publish the workload, hardware, costs, errors, and limits. “Millions of agents” has little meaning without a definition of what they are doing and how reliably the system handles it.

The release sequence should begin with one complete journey: enter the app, understand a task, use permitted information, approve its cost and effects, execute, receive a correct record, and recover from a failure. Direct member governance, a full distribution cycle, and an independent security review follow before live financial dependence expands. More elaborate automation and immersive interfaces can grow from a foundation that has demonstrated its boundaries.

Technology makes these boundaries enforceable. Usability makes them available to the person whose life and work they protect. Their value is finally expressed in the habits the app encourages: whether people understand one another more clearly, act with greater confidence, and remain free to shape the systems they inhabit.

V. Culture — A World Worth Participating In

A system teaches through repetition. It teaches people what to notice, which contributions matter, how disagreement is handled, and what must be traded for belonging. Over time, the interface, the compensation rules, and the small habits of participation become part of the culture.

Kiduna's cultural purpose is to make capable participation feel possible. People should encounter a world in which they can ask a question without performing expertise, contribute without becoming a product, disagree without becoming an enemy, and receive help without surrendering authorship. These possibilities depend on everyday design choices as much as on a constitutional statement.

Arriving with a purpose

Most people will arrive because something matters to them. They want to finish a piece of work, learn a subject, find a collaborator, join an event, or understand a problem. The app should begin there. It can reveal more structure as the person needs it.

A useful first experience might be a document that becomes easier to understand, a question that reaches the right person, a task that moves forward, or an introduction accepted by both parties. It should establish the practical value of participation before asking for a substantial financial commitment or extensive personal disclosure.

The language of the interface should be familiar enough to act on. People, projects, organizations, documents, tasks, payments, and decisions are already meaningful. Internal architecture can remain precise without requiring every participant to adopt a new vocabulary. A person should know what a control will do before learning why the system was organized that way.

Progressive disclosure helps people grow into capability. A simple task can have a simple presentation, with evidence, history, permissions, and accounting available when needed. The advanced view is not a different truth. It is a fuller account of the same work.

Attention in service of intention

The app should bring forward what matters to the person in the current context: an approaching commitment, a decision they are entitled to make, a response to their work, or an opportunity related to their purpose. It should give a reason for the suggestion and allow the person to change the conditions that produced it.

Relevance, urgency, confidence, consequence, and personal interest are different judgments. Combining them into one hidden ranking makes the system difficult to question. A widely discussed item may be irrelevant to someone's task. A quiet obligation may matter greatly. What deserves immediate interruption should be governed separately from what is worth exploring later.

Natural stopping points are part of a usable app. A completed task should be allowed to feel complete. Notifications need quiet periods and manageable settings. A person's refusal should not trigger escalating persuasion. Paid placement or sponsorship must be identified wherever it affects what is presented.

The same discipline applies to financial prompts. Low balances can be explained without urgency theater. A refill can be authorized without obscuring its consequences. A token price change should not become a reason to pressure people into holding more than their intended use requires.

Intelligence with a recognizable relationship

People should know when they are communicating with a person, when a person is being represented by software, and when software is generating a response from available material. A familiar voice or appearance must not conceal authorship or imply that the represented person personally reviewed every message.

An assistant can develop continuity through knowledge a person has chosen to share, preferences they can inspect, and responsibilities they have assigned. That continuity should make the person more capable. It must not become a claim that the software knows their hidden needs better than they do or has earned authority to override their choices.

The app can invite correction in ordinary ways: “That is not what I meant,” “Do not remember this,” “Use this only for this project,” or “Ask me before sending.” The underlying controls must give those statements concrete effect. A warm conversation cannot compensate for a permission system that ignores them.

Representations of places, ideas, stories, or historical figures may support learning and imagination. Their sources and interpretive status must remain clear. A speaking river can be a meaningful artistic experience without becoming evidence of the river's intentions or an independent authority over a real-world decision.

Recognition without a hierarchy of human worth

People contribute in ways that are easy to count and ways that are easy to miss. A finished tool is visible. So is a sale. Maintenance, patient explanation, conflict repair, careful review, and the prevention of a bad decision may be less visible while making everything else possible.

Compensation and recognition should leave room for these contributions without pretending that every kind of value can be reduced to a single score. A person may be highly experienced in one domain and learning in another. A useful record of a contribution should not become a permanent rank attached to the whole person.

The app should help participants understand how credit is assigned, how a method or document was derived, what rights apply, and which people made the work possible. Attribution must distinguish authorship, maintenance, instruction, inspiration, and financial support. An automated system does not become the owner of work merely because it helped produce it.

Licenses, permissions, and compensation terms should travel with reusable material. Cultural knowledge and traditional practices may have expectations that a generic software license does not capture. Where use is contested or rights are unclear, the system should retain that uncertainty and offer review rather than convert availability into permission.

Disagreement and repair

A participatory system needs more than a way to register approval. It needs a way to remain in relationship when purposes diverge. Someone may question a proposal's evidence, object to its consequences, or conclude that the organization is moving in a direction they cannot support.

The app should make these differences intelligible without turning every dispute into a public performance. It can separate a request for clarification from an allegation, a factual correction from a value disagreement, and a temporary protective measure from a final sanction. It should preserve the relevant evidence and let each person respond in an appropriate setting.

Moderation must have a published mandate. People need to know what conduct is prohibited, who can intervene, what evidence is considered, and how a decision can be appealed. Software may assist by surfacing a pattern or an inconsistency. A model-generated suspicion is not a finding of misconduct.

Appeals should have a reachable human owner, a reasonable response period, conflict-of-interest rules, and remedies that can actually be implemented. The person affected should see the reason for a restriction and its scope. A problem in one activity should not automatically erase unrelated participation or access to personal records.

Repair can include correcting a record, acknowledging harm, changing a policy, returning funds, revising a permission, or ending a relationship responsibly. The aim is not to make disagreement disappear. It is to preserve the capacity to choose deliberately after something has gone wrong.

The freedom to leave

Voluntary participation is more credible when departure is practical. People should be able to pause notifications, revoke delegation, cancel future automatic funding, export their own permitted information, and leave an organization through a process they can understand.

Exit does not erase debts, completed transactions, other people's rights, or legitimate retention obligations. It also does not automatically grant a departing member a share of the treasury. These limits should be explained when people join, not discovered when they try to leave.

An organization can choose a different infrastructure operator or form a new undertaking under explicit agreements. Its members may preserve relationships even when a particular structure ends. The software should support continuity of permitted work and identity without treating every departure as a failure to be prevented.

This freedom improves the meaning of continued participation. People staying because the app is useful and the relationships matter is stronger evidence than retention produced by inaccessible exports, lost context, or financial entanglement.

Accessibility as practical agency

An interface that formally permits participation while making it unreadable or exhausting has preserved the right only on paper. Text must remain legible, controls reachable, focus visible, and information available without dependence on color, sound, animation, or a precise pointing device.

The proposed app should support keyboard and screen-reader use, text enlargement, reduced motion, captions or transcripts, and low-bandwidth participation. Voice can make interaction easier for some people while creating privacy or accessibility difficulties for others. It should be an available mode with equivalent alternatives, not the only way to exercise a right.

Long proposals need useful summaries and clear routes to the full record. Complex payments need a readable explanation of amount, recipient, purpose, and reversibility. Error states should say what happened and how to recover. A person should not need technical support to understand whether their money moved.

Warm Black Aurora gives the app a visual foundation: a warm black ground, plum surfaces, blue-green panels, restrained violet light, and clear turquoise-to-violet primary actions. Goudy supplies display character; Avenir carries readable body text. The design should help people distinguish structure, emphasis, and action while preserving generous space for reading and thought. Atmosphere serves orientation; it does not excuse low contrast or decorative distraction.

Consistency matters across the product. The same work should retain its identity when opened from a search, a project, a conversation, or a notification. A familiar action should behave predictably. A changed permission or irreversible effect should be conspicuous enough to notice. The app earns trust through these repeated small experiences.

Living with differences

A research team, a creative production, a family project, a professional association, and a watershed organization will not share every norm. They may use different styles of discussion, rhythms of work, compensation methods, and forms of celebration. The architecture should support this variety while keeping basic rights and cross-boundary obligations legible.

Shared protocols establish how commitments, permissions, and payments can be understood across those differences. They do not require cultural sameness. A connection can be useful precisely because the parties retain distinct knowledge and ways of working.

The same applies to ecological responsibility. Work consumes energy, materials, attention, and time, and its effects may travel beyond the people who approved it. Where resource-use data is available, it should be reported with method and uncertainty. Where it is absent, the system should say so. Care for a place cannot be established by an invented sustainability score.

People can appoint accountable stewards to bring neglected interests into decisions, including downstream effects and future obligations. Their judgments remain attributable and supported by evidence where factual claims are made. Moral commitments and measured outcomes should inform one another without being confused.

What success would feel like, and how to test it

A person should leave a useful session with greater understanding, a completed piece of work, a clearer decision, or a relationship they can act through. An organization should become better able to coordinate without making its members less informed or less free. These experiences can be studied without turning private life into a hidden behavioral profile.

Product evaluation should combine observable outcomes with voluntary reports. How long did it take to reach the first useful result? Did the task finish correctly? Did the person understand what they authorized and paid? Could they find and change the permission afterward? Did the group make and execute a decision with less repeated work? Could someone disagree, appeal, or exit successfully?

Those measures belong alongside service reliability, real paid use, financial resilience, and concentration of compensation and delegated authority. No single number captures success. A higher volume of actions can coexist with lower agency; a slower decision can be appropriate when consequences deserve attention.

Pilots should compare recurring work before and after adoption, disclose sample sizes, subsidies, attrition, and uncertainty, and separate satisfied early enthusiasts from a broader population. The first event, the first paid project, the first contested vote, the first refund, and the first migration each test a different part of the promise.

The initial TGE should fund the ability to demonstrate this complete experience. Before it proceeds, the responsible bodies need verified legal terms, a reconciled supply and distribution plan, documented wallet and reserve authorities, an executable liquidity design, funded operations, independent security review, and a working path from human authorization to accountable execution. Those are conditions for the launch, not achievements implied by this paper.

After launch, the more durable obligation is to keep learning. Publish what works, what fails, where control remains concentrated, and what members have chosen to change. Treat adoption as evidence to examine, not a verdict that the design can no longer be questioned.

The economic system supplies the means to act. Governance keeps the right to decide with people. Technology carries those decisions into the world. Culture determines whether the experience strengthens the relationships and lives from which the work begins.

The app brings these commitments into an ordinary encounter: something worth understanding, someone worth meeting, work worth doing, and a next action that remains yours to choose.
